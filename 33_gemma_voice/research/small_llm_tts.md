# Small language-model TTS: evidence and implications for Gemma Voice

Research date: 2026-10-30. Scope: published papers, official model cards and repositories, mostly 2024–2026. A “0.5B TTS” label often counts the language model and omits a codec, flow model, or vocoder. These results are not comparable without matching test text, speakers, prompts, ASR, normalization, sampling and listening protocol. **UTMOS/Squim-MOS are automatic predictions, not human MOS.** WER/CER are percentages unless otherwise stated. “Not reported” below means not found in the cited sources, not proof that no value exists.

## Executive findings

1. **Data and pretrained speech components dominate the apparent size story.** Spark uses 102.5k hours, Llasa 250k, CosyVoice 2 about 167k and Kyutai 2.5 million hours, in addition to codec training. Their success is not evidence that a text-only Gemma can learn equivalent TTS from 19 hours. NVIDIA's T5-TTS is a valuable smaller-data counterpoint: 1.8k hours, 220M excluding embeddings, explicit alignment, and a separately pretrained codec; S5 uses 845 real hours plus 3,827 synthetic hours from a trained teacher. Neither is a 19-hour result.
2. **Representations change the problem.** Llasa/NeuTTS emit one *joint semantic-acoustic* token per frame. Spark separates global voice information from a single semantically informed stream. CosyVoice/Chatterbox predict semantic tokens and outsource fine acoustics to a trained flow renderer. CSM/Kyutai keep Mimi RVQ but use a separately trained depth transformer. None establishes that 16 weakly learned parallel Mimi heads will work from scratch on one speaker in a weekend.
3. **Alignment is a first-class intervention.** T5-TTS's prior plus CTC improves a controlled baseline; its CTC without the prior failed. OuteTTS 0.2 injects word alignment into serialized examples; S5 learns incremental word alignment. Alignment can be tested before an expensive architecture migration.
4. **Mimi's CB0 is not a standalone waveform codec.** The local 97% CB0 reconstruction CER and 18.6% four-codebook CER rule out treating CB0 audio as an intelligibility target by itself. A semantic-token-to-mel renderer would need training or a compatible pretrained renderer; simply emitting CB0 cannot unlock the full 32-codebook decoder.
5. **Do the local comparison first.** Measure codec round trips with the independent ASR, then a matched-budget learned model; the local LJ CTC score is a biased proxy and cannot be compared with published WER. MLX availability below is a source- or background-level claim, not a test in this research task.

### Reading token rates correctly

“Frames/s” is the number of temporal backbone steps only if codebooks are predicted in parallel or by a depth decoder. “Scalar tokens/s” is frames/s × codebooks if all are emitted; serialized codebooks can multiply the number of autoregressive steps. For example, Mimi 12.5 frames/s × 32 = 400 code indices/s, but the time backbone need not run 400 times/s. XCodec2 has 50 frames/s × one 65,536-way index = 50 AR steps/s and a large output softmax. Codec round-trip fidelity and *predictability from text* are different measurements.

## Model-by-model evidence

### OuteTTS 0.2 500M and OuteTTS 1.0 1B

- **Architecture/input/alignment:** [0.2 model card](https://huggingface.co/OuteAI/OuteTTS-0.2-500M): Qwen2.5-0.5B decoder, WavTokenizer (one codebook, 75 tokens/s), text and speech serialized with speech prompts; the card credits CTC forced alignment. The developer described the 0.1 format as interleaving word text, duration and corresponding speech tokens ([original announcement](https://www.reddit.com/r/LocalLLaMA/comments/1gjak4a)); the 0.2 card says the approach was maintained. Thus forced alignment is a data-preparation dependency, not an architecture-free improvement. 1.0 uses Llama 3.2 1B and [IBM DAC speech 1.5 kbps, two codebooks, 150 serialized tokens/s](https://huggingface.co/OuteAI/Llama-OuteTTS-1.0-1B); its prompt and automatic text-alignment behavior changed, so do not transfer the 0.2 format uncritically. Both use the respective LLM's subword tokenizer; exact normalization and alignment training details require code inspection.
- **Data/compute:** 0.2 reports over 5 billion audio prompt tokens and names Emilia, LibriTTS-R and MLS, but not usable unique hours or GPU-hours. 1.0 reports ~60k hours, 1M-token batch, 8,192 context, AdamW LR 3e-4→3e-5; subsequent 10k high-quality examples at 1e-5→5e-6. Hardware/time not reported. There is also a Qwen3 0.6B 1.0 variant; do not confuse it with 0.2 500M.
- **Inference/quality:** 1.0 recommends ~10 s voice reference, temperature 0.4, top-k 40, top-p 0.9, min-p 0.05, repetition penalty 1.1 restricted to **last 64 tokens**; full-context repetition penalties reportedly break output. No comparable WER/CER or human MOS located in the official cards. Official [repository](https://github.com/edwko/OuteTTS) lists MLX-Audio support for 1.0; not verified locally.
- **Transfer:** word-level supervision is interesting; forcing 150 serial acoustic predictions/s and retokenizing this whole pipeline is a substantial change. The 10k-example tuning stage starts from a 60k-hour speech-pretrained model.

### Spark-TTS 0.5B

- **Architecture:** Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct autoregressively models [BiCodec](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.01710): one 50 Hz semantic VQ stream (8,192 entries in the reported setup) plus 32 fixed-length global speaker tokens. Semantic inputs derive from pretrained wav2vec2 features; the learned waveform decoder combines semantic and global streams. Attributes can be generated in a structured sequence; ordinary text uses Qwen text tokens. Alignment is implicit in text/prompt-to-speech sequence training, not forced alignment.
- **Data/compute:** VoxBox 102,500 hours English/Chinese after curation, including ASR-based filtering; codec training uses about 3,000 hours in the paper. GPU-hours not found. The decoder is a substantial separately trained component.
- **Quality/inference:** On SEED test-en, 1.98% WER, speaker similarity 0.584; test-zh 1.20% CER. On the same paper's tests, no reference prefix improved English WER 1.98→1.32 but reduced speaker similarity 0.584→0.474. UTMOS is automatic; no comparable human MOS or small-data adaptation result located. [Paper tables](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.01710) report additional codec tests.
- **Transfer:** the useful hypothesis is *disentangle voice from the content stream*, not “50 Hz is always better.” A 50 Hz single-codebook target is 4× Mimi's time steps and requires codec encoding and a compatible decoder.

### NeuTTS Air and Nano

- **Architecture:** [NeuTTS training guide](https://github.com/neuphonic/neutts/blob/main/TRAINING.md) specifies Qwen2.5-0.5B and a Llasa-style text/speech sequence. English text is phonemized (eSpeak), and [NeuCodec](https://github.com/neuphonic/neucodec) is one 50 Hz codebook, 24 kHz, 0.8 kbps (16 bits/index, hence 65,536 possible indices). A short reference transcript/audio prefix conditions continuation; alignment is learned from sequence pairs, with no explicit duration alignment described. A 120M Nano exists, but do not assume Air results transfer to it.
- **Data/compute/quality:** [Air card](https://huggingface.co/neuphonic/neutts-air) lists Emilia and NeuCodec-encoded Emilia-YODAS but no total hours, hardware, comparable WER/CER, or human MOS. The repository's practical fine-tuning guidance is LR 1e-5–4e-5 and 1,000–2,000 steps for 10 hours, **for an already speech-trained Air checkpoint**, not text-only Qwen. The guide's examples use PyTorch/Transformers; MLX training compatibility is not established.
- **Transfer:** phoneme input is a low-cost hypothesis to test, but sparse IPA embedding initialization can destroy the pretrained lexical mapping. Preserve word boundaries and compare equal budgets with existing character input.

### Kani-TTS 370M

- **Architecture:** [official card](https://huggingface.co/nineninesix/kani-tts-370m/blob/4ed48fcb2e25c6abea57a3e1655545e2fead66f8/README.md) says LiquidAI LFM2-350M backbone, 370M released model, NVIDIA NanoCodec and 22 kHz output; text is the LFM tokenizer and alignment appears learned in the token sequence. The card does not establish the exact codebook count, frame rate, serialization, or a forced aligner. LFM's hybrid architecture is not Gemma.
- **Data/compute:** card reports ~80k hours from LibriTTS, Common Voice and Emilia; 8 H100 × 45 h = **360 H100-hours**, if the reported wall time covers all eight throughout. This is a provider claim, not independently verified. Fine-tuned voice datasets are listed without a controlled hour/quality curve.
- **Metrics:** card claims MOS 4.3/5 and WER <5%, but does not specify test set, judge, protocol or confidence intervals; treat as non-comparable. It reports 15 s in ~1 s on RTX 5080. Fine-tuning instructions exist, but no reproducible small-data result found. Card metadata and prose disagree on license; check upstream before reuse.

### Llasa 1B

- **Architecture:** [paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.04128) initializes Llama 3.2 1B; XCodec2 combines semantic and acoustic representations into one 65,536-entry quantizer at 50 Hz ([T5Gemma codec description](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.01760)). Llama subword text precedes audio; loss is on speech tokens and alignment emerges autoregressively, without durations. Prompt speech and transcript support zero-shot continuation.
- **Data/compute:** 250k h English/Mandarin, 3 epochs, 2M-token batch, peak LR 5e-5, 3% of an epoch warmup, 2,048-token max length. 80k and 160k-hour variants are also evaluated. Total GPU-hours not specified. [Training and fine-tuning code](https://github.com/zhenye234/LLaSA_training) is available; low-data fine-tune success not quantified in the paper.
- **Quality:** SEED English WER 3.71→3.60→3.22% for 80k→160k→250k h (1B), Chinese CER 2.69→2.22→1.89%. Codec reconstruction itself yields 2.91% English WER vs human 2.14% under their judge. Their inference-time speaker-verifier search and final WER verifier improve selected candidates at substantial extra compute; selecting on ASR can also overfit the verifier and must be reported separately from single-sample performance.

### Orpheus, including announced smaller variants

- **Architecture:** released [Orpheus 3B](https://huggingface.co/canopylabs/orpheus-3b-0.1-ft) is Llama-based, autoregressively emits [SNAC](https://github.com/hubertsiuzdak/snac) multi-scale acoustic codes from text/subword sequences with voice and expressive tags; alignment is learned. 24 kHz SNAC uses hierarchical levels; the developer describes roughly 83 serial tokens/s for Orpheus ([developer discussion](https://www.reddit.com/r/LocalLLaMA/comments/1jo88lg/part_of_orpheus_team_here_ama_educational_content/)). This is a developer statement, not a controlled benchmark.
- **Release caveat:** the [official repository](https://github.com/canopyai/Orpheus-TTS) lists 150M, 400M and 1B as a release checklist; its [engine code](https://github.com/canopyai/Orpheus-TTS/blob/main/orpheus_tts_pypi/orpheus_tts/engine_class.py) labels these unsupported. I did not find authoritative public checkpoints or metrics for them; do not cite 3B performance as proof of small Orpheus performance.
- **Data/compute/quality:** hours, precise base-model training recipe and MOS are not established in the official material examined. The developer states ~1,500 H100-hours for 3B pretraining, with no full accounting; [Kyutai's independent benchmark](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.08753) reports 3.51% English WER on its short-form long-document evaluation. Official [pretraining guide](https://github.com/canopyai/Orpheus-TTS/blob/main/pretrain/readme.md) suggests initially mixing text and speech batches, then shifting toward speech; it warns the example code may have bugs. Fine-tuning code exists; no small-data controlled outcome found.

### Sesame CSM-1B

- **Architecture:** [official code](https://github.com/SesameAILabs/csm) uses a Llama-family temporal backbone and a much smaller depth decoder; Mimi at 12.5 frames/s produces 32 RVQ indices/frame. The temporal network predicts CB0, then the depth transformer autoregressively predicts remaining codebooks conditional on the frame and temporal hidden state. This is a hierarchy, not 32 independent heads. Text is Llama-tokenized and paired with speaker-marked audio turns; text-to-speech alignment is learned implicitly. [Model card](https://huggingface.co/sesame/csm-1b) describes architecture and limitations.
- **Data/compute/quality:** training hours and GPU-hours not disclosed in the inspected source. Kyutai's comparison reports 3.82% English WER in its short-form evaluation; naturalness MOS and small-data fine-tuning outcome not found. Conversational context can include prior text/audio turns. Mimi alone is not a renderer for its first codebook; the depth model has to learn the rest.
- **Transfer:** CSM is the clearest architectural explanation for why a strong separately trained acoustic stage might help, but replicating depth autoregression adds up to 31 serial substeps per frame and its own training problem. A community MLX conversion exists, but compatibility and quality are unverified here.

### Kyutai TTS “1.6B” / delayed streams

- **Size/representation:** despite its name, the [official model card](https://huggingface.co/kyutai/tts-1.6b-en_fr) says **1.8B actual**, including ~1B temporal backbone and ~600M depth transformer (other components/embeddings account for the difference); not a pretrained Gemma. Mimi is 12.5 Hz, 32 codebooks (400 indices/s), hierarchical time/depth generation. The card describes text aligned ahead of audio by 16 frames = 1.28 s, plus two-step semantic/acoustic delay. Its prose accidentally describes STT in one paragraph; the TTS-specific architecture and [DSM paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.08753) settle the direction.
- **Alignment:** forced/pseudo word timestamps place text in a time stream, with learned padding/action and future text lookahead so the model can start speaking before the whole sentence. The delay is **between text and audio**, different from MusicGen's inter-codebook delay. This is a different training representation, not a change to inference alone.
- **Scale/results:** 2.5M h audio with Whisper-medium timestamps/transcripts; 750k steps, batch 64, 120 s segments, then 24k CFG-distillation updates. Hardware/GPU-hours not stated in card. Paper long-form English average WER 1.72%, French 2.96%; short-form turn-by-turn English 2.06%. The authors report subjective evaluations separately. The paper's controlled codec comparison gives Mimi 32-level WER 1.68% vs EnCodec 8-level 2.45% in its setup; both have fully trained models and cannot be compared to our weak four-head checkpoint. Fewer RVQ levels can be used at inference, trading quality for speed. Fine-tuning on small data not quantified.
- **MLX:** background states `moshi_mlx` already runs Mimi locally; that does not demonstrate Kyutai TTS training support.

### Chatterbox 0.5B

- **Architecture:** [official repository](https://github.com/resemble-ai/chatterbox) and [S3Gen code](https://github.com/resemble-ai/chatterbox/blob/master/src/chatterbox/models/s3gen/s3gen.py) show a Llama-style T3 speech-token LM plus supervised S3 speech tokenizer at **25 Hz**, and a token-to-mel conditional flow renderer plus HiFT waveform generator and voice conditioning. T3 uses a text tokenizer and learned text-to-semantic alignment. 0.5B is a model-family headline, not the end-to-end computational budget. Flow is essential to recovering acoustic detail.
- **Data/compute/quality:** A [downstream model card](https://huggingface.co/CoRal-project/roest-v3-chatterbox-500m) attributes >500k hours to multilingual Chatterbox; I could not verify the exact hours and variant in the official repository, so treat this as secondary reporting. Detailed split, compute, controlled small-data fine-tune and human MOS protocol were not located. [Kyutai paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.08753) independently reports 1.95% WER on its English short-form long-document setting. Exaggeration and CFG allow style/accuracy tuning; watermarking is included in the release. Do not equate this score with LJ CER.
- **Transfer:** predicting content-rich tokens plus using a pretrained compatible renderer is plausible; using S3 tokens without S3Gen is not a complete TTS pipeline. MLX-Audio implementations may be usable for evaluation; installed version/model support and decoding parity must be checked.

### CosyVoice 2 / 3

- **Architecture:** [CosyVoice 2 paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.10117) uses a Qwen-based ~0.5B text-to-semantic autoregressive LM and separate chunk-aware conditional flow-matching acoustic decoder plus vocoder. Supervised Whisper-derived semantic tokens with FSQ are 25 Hz in the released configuration; text uses BPE directly without G2P; multi-character Chinese tokens are masked so Chinese characters are encoded separately, while English/Japanese/Korean are not subject to this restriction. It trains both text-first and interleaved text/speech sequences for offline and streaming generation, with alignment learned in the sequence. The flow module supplies detail that semantic indices do not contain.
- **Data/compute:** paper lists 130k h Chinese, 30k English, 4.6k Japanese, 2.2k Korean = **166.8k h** for TTS, and ~210.8k h for tokenizer supervision. GPU-hours not stated. CosyVoice 3's [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.17589) describes scaling from 0.5B to 1.5B and ~1M h; a later [Fun-CosyVoice3 0.5B release](https://github.com/FunAudioLLM/CosyVoice) exists. Do not assume the 1.5B paper's full data or metrics apply automatically to that checkpoint. The new tokenizer is supervised on ASR, language, emotion, events and speaker tasks.
- **Quality/fine-tuning:** CosyVoice 2 SEED test-en WER 2.57%, Chinese CER 1.45% in [Llasa's table](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.04128). [CosyVoice 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.10117) reports reasonable speaker adaptation with as few as 400 recordings from an already pretrained model, multispeaker SFT at LR 1e-5, then optional ASR/speaker reward optimization. It does not claim that 400 recordings suffice to learn TTS from text-only initialization. Chunked flow makes streaming possible; solver steps and lookahead affect latency and quality.

### VibeVoice-Realtime 0.5B

- **Architecture:** [Microsoft's card](https://huggingface.co/microsoft/VibeVoice-Realtime-0.5B) names Qwen2.5-0.5B and a separately pretrained continuous acoustic tokenizer; a ~40M, four-layer diffusion head predicts continuous latent vectors conditioned on LM hidden states. The Realtime card specifies 7.5 Hz acoustic features and explicitly removes the semantic tokenizer used in other family models; the [technical report](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.19205) describes the broader family. There is no discrete RVQ codebook count for the continuous acoustic output. Text uses Qwen subwords; streaming text/audio alignment is learned in training, exact supervision not fully detailed in the card.
- **Data/compute/results:** card does not disclose hours or GPU-hours. Acoustic tokenizer is frozen while LM and diffusion head train, with 4k→8k sequence curriculum. Reported LibriSpeech test-clean WER 2.00%, similarity 0.695; SEED test-en WER 2.05%, similarity 0.633. Naturalness human MOS and small-data fine-tuning result not located. Precomputed voice/KV embeddings, CFG and diffusion steps are inference tradeoffs; verify cloning restrictions for the exact release.
- **Transfer:** 7.5 steps/s is attractive but next-token diffusion plus training a compatible latent decoder is a different and larger project than a codec-head swap.

### NVIDIA T5-TTS (2024)

- **Architecture/input:** [paper](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2024/neekhara24_interspeech.pdf): pretrained T5 encoder and decoder, 12 layers each, 768 hidden, **220M excluding embeddings**. It compares SentencePiece and phoneme inputs, and 3 s speaker context. EnCodec 75 frames/s × 8 RVQ, DAC 86 × 9 RVQ, or FSQ spectral codec 86 × 8 independent codebooks. The first two use delayed RVQ; independent spectral codebooks can be parallel heads. Codec is pretrained; waveform rendering is separate.
- **Alignment:** beta-binomial monotonic prior biases text cross-attention; CTC loss on attention encourages a monotonic path. No ground-truth durations required. On 200 held-out seen-speaker LibriTTS utterances with spectral codec, no prior/CTC: **4.01% CER / 6.74% WER**; prior alone 2.56/4.68; prior+CTC 2.16/3.91. With CTC but no prior the authors report monotonic yet unaligned attention and no synthesis. This is an ablation on an encoder-decoder cross-attention system, not direct proof of the same gain in Gemma decoder-only attention.
- **Data/compute/quality:** 1.8k h including LibriTTS, HiFiTTS, MLS and 63 proprietary hours; batch 192 across 32 A100s, 250k steps, LR 1e-4, top-k 80/temperature 0.85 at inference. Wall time not stated. Spectral codec seen-speaker human MOS is 3.91±0.05 in its codec comparison; the challenging-text evaluation reports 4.06±0.038 on a different sentence subset. No low-data adaptation curve. See full table for different speaker conditions and exact MOS.

### NVIDIA Koel-TTS (2025)

- **Architecture:** [paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.05236) extends T5-style encoder-decoder codec LM with parallel independent acoustic codebook prediction, multiple speaker-conditioning approaches, monotonic prior and attention CTC. Multilingual version is **1.1B**; do not assume that all reported smaller English architecture sizes are 1.1B. It compares IPA phonemes and characters, using characters for languages without the applicable phonemizer. The low-frame-rate FSQ codec has eight independent codebooks at 21.5 frames/s, 172 scalar indices/s and 1.89 kbps, with a separately trained decoder.
- **Data/compute:** 18k h English; 21k h in multilingual blend; training hardware/time not established here. Preference pairs use ASR and speaker-verifier rewards; classifier-free guidance requires conditional dropout in training and two conditional/unconditional evaluations at inference. That compute is not free.
- **Quality:** in the paper's baseline table, seen-speaker SV-conditioned model has 1.71% CER, 2.82% WER and **automatic** Squim-MOS 4.36; preference optimization and CFG have separate results. Fine-tuning is preference alignment on pretrained speech models, not a demonstrated 19-hour cold start.

### NVIDIA S5-TTS (2026)

- **Architecture:** [paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.21882): 160M encoder-decoder, 4 encoder and 8 decoder layers, 768 hidden. IPA phoneme inputs; pretrained NeMo FSQ mel codec, eight independent codebooks at 86.1 frames/s (8 indices per frame; paper describes 80 bits/frame, 6.9 kbps). Word-level lookahead-causal masks and auxiliary monotonic attention/CTC support incremental generation. This is a separate trained codec/renderer, not just 160M total.
- **Data/compute:** 845.04 h real LibriTTS+HiFiTTS, plus 3,827.5 h ASR-filtered teacher-generated speech in distillation. 4 B200s, effective batch 128, 250k training steps; 100k further distillation steps. It uses zero-WER filtering for generated text and both soft labels and ground truth. Wall-clock GPU-hours not specified; teacher training and synthetic generation are additional costs.
- **Results:** 200 unseen LibriTTS utterances, Parakeet judge: k=2 baseline 3.49% WER, UTMOS 3.66; with distillation 2.65%, UTMOS 3.72, **human MOS 3.71±0.062**. Full-context teacher: 3.20% WER, human MOS 3.75±0.064. No small-data fine-tuning experiment found. The 845 real hours + distilled teacher provide a useful data-efficient direction, but cannot be counted as 845 hours alone.

### Aratako T5Gemma-TTS

- **Scope:** [technical report](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.01760), [repository](https://github.com/Aratako/T5Gemma-TTS). Released/evaluated model is **T5Gemma 2B encoder + 2B decoder**, approximately 5B checkpoint, not a 270M T5Gemma 2 model. It uses SentencePiece text with no phonemizer, XCodec2 single 65,536-way index at 50 Hz, and persistent encoder cross-attention. PM-RoPE conditions desired length; reference tokens give speaker context. The default codec decoder was fine-tuned on Japanese while the encoder remains compatible with original XCodec2.
- **Data/compute:** ~170k h (100k English, 50k Chinese, 20k Japanese) Emilia + LibriHeavy; GPU-hours not found. No reported small-data adaptation experiment. Its duration control and cross-attention are architecture inspiration, not evidence the 270M port will work within the weekend.
- **Quality:** Japanese CER 12.6%, Korean CER 8.2% using Whisper large-v3 in that report. English evaluation overlaps LibriHeavy/LibriSpeech and is explicitly labeled an upper-bound estimate by authors. Japanese speaker similarity 0.677 vs XTTS v2 0.622; this is not MOS. The reported RTF 0.8–2.0 is setup-specific. Duration accuracy 0.79 vs 0.46 without PM-RoPE under their protocol.

### Newer small example: Ming-3.0-TTS 0.5B

- [Official card](https://huggingface.co/inclusionAI/Ming-3.0-tts-0.5B) identifies Qwen2.5-0.5B, a DiT head, audio tokenizer and speaker encoder, 44.1 kHz output, plus language/style controls. The decoder and tokenizer are included in the roughly 3 GB checkpoint, so “0.5B” is not a full-stack size comparison. Precise token rate/codebook structure, alignment supervision, training hours and compute were not established from the inspected primary card; do not infer these from another model's config. It reports SEED test-en 1.584% WER, speaker similarity 0.691; naturalness and small-data adaptation not established here.
- A [community MLX bf16 conversion](https://huggingface.co/mlx-community/Ming-3.0-tts-0.5B-bf16) reports M4 Pro RTF ~1.0 and says mlx-audio **0.6.1+** is needed because 0.6.0 and earlier can speak the wrong words. These are conversion-author claims, not locally verified, and are a useful warning to test tokenizer/prompt parity before using any port as a judge.

## What transfers, and what does not

### 1. Semantic content and acoustic detail are different targets

Llasa folds both into one large vocabulary; Spark disentangles global speaker data; CSM uses hierarchical depth; Chatterbox and CosyVoice depend on an independently trained acoustic renderer; VibeVoice predicts continuous latents with a diffusion head. A system can look simple at its LLM interface because difficult work was moved into codec pretraining. A fair local codec bake-off must record both **round-trip** and **generated** speech, token rate, model sizes and wall-clock. The best reconstructed codec need not be the easiest to predict. At 50 Hz, 19 h yields 3.42 million speech indices per epoch; Mimi at 12.5 Hz yields 855k frames/epoch, each with multiple target indices. For 65,536 classes × 640 hidden, a separate output matrix has ~41.9M weights (about 84 MB bf16 weights, before gradients/optimizer); do not use a 262k-wide text softmax for speech unnecessarily.

### 2. Time structure is supervision

Oute's word-aligned serialization, T5's prior+CTC and DSM's timestamped streams each put structure into training. A decoder-only causal prefix does not automatically guarantee robust monotonic alignment. Existing local evidence that swapped text hurts CB0 loss shows conditioning, not that the model follows every word. **Inference:** because trained audio lasts many more steps than the text prefix and Gemma has 512-token local attention in five out of six layers, long samples could make text access more difficult; global layers retain access, so this is a testable hypothesis, not proof of the actual failure. Measure text-attention or a controlled length sweep before changing masks.

### 3. More examples and better labels often beat clever decoding

The 19-hour plateau and train/validation divergence are compatible with overfitting, though not proof of its only cause. Add LibriTTS-R and Hi-Fi only after checking splits, transcripts, sample rate, codec/reconstruction and speaker IDs. Track speaker balance and quality. Voice ID conditioning avoids accidental averaging across speakers. A few hundred clips can adapt a *speech-pretrained* model; it does not imply comparable training from Gemma text weights. Authors' benchmark data include strong codec pretraining and much larger duration.

### 4. Low temperature is not an alignment cure

Constrained sampling can improve intelligibility and reduce diversity, while repetition penalties may cause omissions. Test greedy and temperatures 0.2, 0.4, 0.7 with fixed seeds; log deletions, insertions and repetitions and listen. Best-of-N selected by ASR is useful as a separately labeled inference-compute baseline, but the selector may exploit the ASR and is not unbiased naturalness evidence. Always evaluate with an independent ASR and a fixed held-out set, including real and codec-round-trip controls.

### 5. MLX and file interchange

Use the existing MLX Gemma and Mimi path; no installation is implied. NeuCodec/XCodec2/BiCodec/S3Gen require verifying that both **encoding and decoding** actually exist in the already installed MLX stack or an allowed separate codec environment before committing. Safe proposed cached representation: per utterance `.npz` containing `codes` (integer `[frames, codebooks]`, exact codebook order), `sample_rate`, `frame_rate`, codec/version ID, normalized text, speaker ID, source/split and audio hash; maintain a JSONL manifest and record exact resampling. Hash or version the codec and preprocessing, and never share utterances or near-duplicates across train/val/test. These are proposed local conventions, not official formats.

## Recommendations for us

The recommendations below are hypotheses and estimated engineering budgets, not measured local results. Keep every run resumable. The brief allows 2–3 h main runs, while the supplied Joy Lab rules cap a single run at **45 minutes**; follow the stricter cap and resume across segments. Verify save/kill/resume including optimizer, step, RNG and config before any segment longer than 20 minutes; use the shared GPU lock and ≤4 CPU threads, resolving the correct lock/venv paths from the project folder. This research task ran no training.

1. **Make the evaluation trustworthy before selecting a new architecture.** **Impact:** very high decision value; not itself an acoustic gain. **Effort:** ~1–3 h engineering/evaluation, subject to existing MLX ASR availability. **Risk:** low, but another recognizer can still be biased. Run an independent Whisper-class recognizer on the identical frozen 100 LJ test clips: real audio, Mimi round trips at 4/8/16/32 codebooks, project 27, and current Gemma at fixed seed/temp 0.2. Record CER **and** WER, ASR normalization, per-utterance insertion/deletion/substitution, duration ratio, failures and 10–20 blinded paired listens; report both judges rather than recalibrating one to the other. Do not use the held-out test to select temperatures/checkpoints: tune on LJ041–044, freeze, then run LJ045–050 once. Treat project 27's 6.2% and the current 38% as different protocol snapshots until rerun identically.

2. **Get more diverse speech into the existing pipeline and keep LJ identity explicit.** **Impact:** high expected potential given local overfit; magnitude unknown. **Effort:** ~3–6 h preparation plus 6–12 GPU-hours across resumable ≤45 min segments, conditional on already encoded data and lock availability. **Risk:** increased speaker variability, data-quality and split leakage. Start with the ~54 h LibriTTS-R clean-100 + 19 h LJ, add learned speaker ID embeddings, balance batches e.g. 50% LJ / 50% Libri, and reserve disjoint speakers/chapters. Compare LJ-only and mixture from the **same** current initialization with equal total training tokens, LR schedule and validation cadence. Then add available verified Hi-Fi speakers (≥17 h each) in an explicit 25% LJ / 50% Libri / 25% Hi-Fi experiment, only after input pipeline checks. Retain local LJ-specific validation and a held-out multispeaker validation. Fine-tune on LJ for the last 10–20% of an equal budget only as a separately reported ablation. The training-hour labels are source duration, not unique information or matched compute.

3. **Run an alignment intervention that fits decoder-only Gemma.** **Impact:** potentially high for deletions/slurring; unproven on this architecture. **Effort:** ~4–8 h engineering and 3–6 GPU-hours for two short arms. **Risk:** forced alignment errors, seams, incorrect train/inference serialization. If a local aligner already exists, align LJ training words and store timestamps/confidences; manually inspect 20 varied samples and discard or flag bad alignments. Construct a clean 1–3-word span experiment with 0.1–0.3 s acoustic context around boundaries, word text plus aligned codec segment and explicit boundary tokens, masking loss on context. **At inference use the identical text/segment protocol**, preserve codec state/context and overlap or stitch only after listening; compare against sentence-level baseline at equal examples/tokens. Alternatively train a small monotonic auxiliary text-position predictor on frames and expose its predicted position during inference—never feed ground-truth positions at inference. Save boundary, long-sentence and repeated-word failures. This is inspired by Oute and S5, not their exact algorithm; a full T5 prior+CTC requires encoder-decoder cross-attention.

4. **Test a single-codebook representation only if its full codec is usable.** **Impact:** high upside, uncertain; **effort:** 2–4 h feasibility/round-trip plus 4–8 h training if already available; **risk:** 4× frame rate and 65,536-class softmax may consume budget and reconstruction may fail. First verify existing MLX or separately permitted NeuCodec/XCodec2 encoder **and decoder**; encode 100 val examples and independently measure round-trip WER/CER, listen, and time encoding. Inspect index utilization and audio duration. If viable, train one Gemma speech head with 65,536 outputs, separate speech embedding, pretrained text pathway unchanged, peak backbone LR 3e-5 / new head 3e-4 as the starting local recipe, fixed token/compute cap matched against Mimi. Compare 19 h and mixed-data variants only if the first short run learns. Do not claim a win solely because codec reconstruction improves. If codec support is missing, log the gap and keep Mimi; do not spend the weekend porting a codec.

5. **Ablate text representation and inference cheaply.** **Impact:** medium, targeted pronunciation; **effort:** 2–4 h plus 2–4 GPU-hours; **risk:** phoneme symbols mapped to newly initialized embeddings lose pretrained text priors and G2P can mispronounce names. Compare existing one-character input to (a) native Gemma BPE, (b) normalized IPA or ARPAbet with word separators **only if an existing local phonemizer is usable**. Keep identical train/val examples and update/token budget; start with LR backbone 3e-5, new embeddings/head 3e-4, then measure distinct-symbol coverage and per-word errors. A combined text+phoneme prompt is an optional third arm, not evidence of benefit. On the same frozen validation checkpoint sweep greedy/0.2/0.4/0.7 and 1–4 candidates; if using ASR reranking, report single-sample and reranked results and verify with the second judge and listening. Avoid full-history repetition penalties.

6. **Consider a compact acoustic refinement stage, but gate it on real codebook evidence.** **Impact:** potentially high on naturalness if higher CBs become predictable; **effort:** 6–12 h engineering/training, high risk in a weekend. Freeze a strong semantic backbone only after it passes alignment, then test a small 2–4 layer depth model predicting CB1–7 conditioned on backbone states and earlier within-frame codes. Teacher-force targets initially; measure each CB's held-out loss/top-1 and free-running speech vs the same parameter/compute budget of independent heads. At generation feed *predicted*, not ground-truth, CB0; Mimi CB0 alone is not an intelligible target. Compute and report additional within-frame serial latency. Do not promise CSM parity; its depth model and audio pretraining are much larger.

7. **Defer T5Gemma 2 port and full streaming redesign until a narrow gate passes.** **Impact:** potentially large alignment control; **effort:** likely >1 day for correct MLX port, weight parity, training and beta-binomial/CTC losses; **risk:** high opportunity cost and no 270M T5Gemma-TTS result. If spare time remains, first demonstrate teacher-forced logits/attention parity on a tiny fixed input against a reference, then train with monotonic prior before adding CTC, reproducing the T5 ablation on local val. Do not assume Aratako's 2B+2B 170k-hour system validates 270M+270M or that duration-free full-sequence training automatically streams. Streaming DSM requires timestamps and a trained action/delay representation, not merely applying MusicGen delay to Mimi.

# Data, training and evaluation for Gemma Voice

Research date: 2026-10-30. Scope: a 270M Gemma decoder generating Mimi codes in MLX on one shared M5 Max; 30–48 GPU-hours. Sources are linked inline. This is a research and planning report, not an executed experiment. Local numbers below come from `BACKGROUND.md`, not independent measurements in this report. Proposed thresholds, budgets and hyperparameters are starting hypotheses, not claims of measured performance.

## Decision

Prioritize an independent ASR evaluation, data integrity and a multi-speaker-to-LJ experiment, with the current single-speaker checkpoint as a matched control. The local 38.0% proxy CER and the 18.6% four-codebook Mimi round-trip proxy CER indicate two separable bottlenecks: generated alignment/content and codec reconstruction. The LJ-specific recognizer is biased, so neither number establishes independent WER or absolute quality. More data may help generalization, but the published multi-speaker results do not guarantee that 54–346 hours and one weekend will beat project 27.

For the primary weekend path retain the existing Gemma port, character input, Mimi and 4 delayed streams for controlled comparisons. Test 8 streams only if reconstruction and generated speech justify their extra head cost. Cross-attention/monotonic alignment is a credible next architecture experiment, but an unported T5Gemma plus a new TTS training stack is too much critical-path risk. Project 27 remains the external baseline, with real and codec-reconstructed speech as references.

## Data and curation

| Corpus | Published facts | Use here |
|---|---|---|
| [LJSpeech](https://keithito.com/LJ-Speech-Dataset/) | 13,100 utterances, ~24 h, one English reader, 22.05 kHz; normalized and original text. | Target voice; preserve the existing chapter split and the exact 100 project-27 test clips. ~19 h training is local accounting, not the full-corpus duration. |
| [LibriTTS-R](https://www.openslr.org/141/) | Restored LibriTTS, ~585 h total, 24 kHz, 2,456 speakers; restoration does not make the audio independent of original LibriTTS. | Start with the local ~54 h train-clean-100; add clean-360 only if prepared in time. Measure post-filter hours and speakers. |
| [Hi-Fi TTS](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2021/bakhturina21_interspeech.pdf) | ~292 h, 10 speakers, at least 17 h each, 44.1 kHz. Source selection targeted SNR >=32 dB in 0.3–4 kHz and bandwidth >=13 kHz; utterances were retained with zero ASR WER. | High-quality, long per-speaker exposure; downsample to Mimi 24 kHz with an anti-aliasing resampler. Original high-frequency content above 12 kHz is unavailable after conversion. |

**Avoid corpus leakage before sampling.** These are overlapping LibriVox/LibriSpeech/Gutenberg families: match source speaker, audiobook/chapter, utterance ID, audio hash/fingerprint and normalized text across LibriTTS, LibriTTS-R and Hi-Fi. LibriTTS-R is a restoration of LibriTTS, not new independent content. A restored waveform can evade byte hashes. Keep LJ val/test chapters excluded from all text and audio used for training and teacher generation. Preserve speaker-disjoint LibriTTS test-clean/test-other and any held-out speaker/source books, taking care that a speaker or their book does not reappear in other corpora. Detect exact and near duplicate transcripts and long common substrings, then audit samples manually. Store split and exclusion manifests before encoding.

**Suggested pipeline**, calibrated on 100 randomly sampled clips per corpus rather than blindly enforcing one global cutoff:

1. Keep original files and source metadata. Verify audio decoding, mono channel, sample rate, finite values, clipping, silence and empty text. Resample once to 24 kHz float/PCM for Mimi. Do not normalize loudness so aggressively that recording conditions or clipped peaks are obscured; log peak and RMS, and conservatively flag e.g. >0.1% samples at digital full scale. Trim only long leading/trailing silence with conservative padding, never truncate speech or excise internal pauses.
2. Retain raw text, dataset normalized text, and a versioned **spoken** training text. Normalize unicode, whitespace, numbers, dates, currency and abbreviations to match what is actually spoken; keep punctuation for prosody. Compare normalized reference and ASR text using the same fixed evaluation normalizer, but never replace the ground truth automatically with an ASR transcript. Audit numbers, names, apostrophes and abbreviations. Keep evaluation normalization separate from the TTS input representation.
3. Start with 1–15 s, 2–40 words and roughly 1–5 words/s as *review flags*, not universal exclusions. Preserve 1–10 s LJ clips and add a bounded long-sentence validation bucket; reject only confirmed truncation, empty speech, severe silence, overlapped speakers or transcription mismatch. Log duration, rate, speech ratio, corpus/speaker and rejected hours. Do not turn a low apparent WER on very short utterances into automatic proof of correctness.
4. ASR-filter with Parakeet or Whisper using normalized word edit distance, no reference-text prompting. Proposed three bins: WER <= 0.10 pass, 0.10–0.20 manual review, >0.20 quarantine; substitutions, insertions and deletions recorded. For short texts use raw edit count too. Audit a stratified sample of all bins, especially names, uncommon accents, restoration artifacts and sentences that differ only by normalization. Zero WER is more conservative but removes hard words and dialects and can preserve correlated ASR errors; Hi-Fi's published zero-WER and S5's synthetic-data filter are examples, not a reason to discard all imperfect real clips.
5. Track SNR only with a defined estimator and speech/noise assumptions; a single crude RMS ratio is not comparable to Hi-Fi's band-specific SNR. Clip-level DNSMOS P.835 OVRL >=3.0 is one *optional review threshold*: [Emilia](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.15907) used this on its original data, and 2.4 on incremental data. It is an intrusive selection bias, not a guarantee of clean speech or accurate text. Record scores, model/version, excluded hours and by-speaker distributions; preserve samples in quarantine and manually listen to borderline examples. Avoid filters that erase particular speakers. A restored LibriTTS-R recording can have enhancement artifacts despite a good score.
6. Encode once with a pinned Mimi checkpoint and settings. A JSONL manifest should contain `id`, `corpus`, `speaker_id`, `book/chapter`, `split`, `audio_path`, `audio_sha256`, `sample_rate`, `duration_s`, `text_raw`, `text_spoken`, `text_normalizer_version`, `asr_model`, `asr_transcript`, `wer`, `quality_scores`, `filter_decision`, `codec_version`, `codes_path`, and `n_frames`. Keep integer `[frames, codebooks]` arrays, explicit dtype and endianness, plus checksums; don't embed large arrays in JSON. Check deterministic round-trip and alignment of frames and transcript. Record net hours by corpus/speaker and effective sampling weights.

## Conditioning and staged recipe

**Speaker ID**: a learned token or embedding per known speaker is cheap and unambiguous, and is the recommended first multi-speaker experiment. It cannot clone a new speaker; allocate a distinct LJ ID and include enough LJ in training or initialize its new embedding during adaptation. Match identities across corpora where confirmed and otherwise keep corpus-qualified IDs.

**Speaker embedding**: a frozen speaker verifier extracts one vector from a separate same-speaker reference, projected into the LM. It can generalize beyond a fixed lookup but requires a verified implementation and may encode channel/style as identity. **Audio prompt**: prepend 2–3 s of codec codes from a *different utterance* of the same speaker, with prompt transcript if the architecture expects it. [VALL-E](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.02111) trained at 60k h and used a 3 s prompt; [T5-TTS](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2024/neekhara24_interspeech.pdf) uses a different utterance as 3 s context. That large-scale evidence does not establish zero-shot cloning at 54 h. Codec prompts cost sequence positions, risk copying prompt words or acoustic conditions, and need prompt/target masking and held-out references. Do not compare an audio-prompt model against a speaker-ID model without reporting the extra information and inference cost.

**Main experiment:** (1) begin from the same text-pretrained Gemma weights and initialized speech modules; train on LibriTTS-R plus LJ, and Hi-Fi when ready, with speaker IDs. Start example sampling 70% multi-speaker / 30% LJ by *speech seconds*; cap oversampling per speaker and log actual hours processed. Reweight if LJ loss dominates or speaker diversity collapses. (2) Resume the best multi-speaker checkpoint into LJ adaptation: 80–90% LJ / 10–20% multi-speaker replay for 1–3 effective LJ passes, then evaluate. Optionally test LJ-only adaptation against replay at matched optimizer steps. Same data format, codec, masks and audio duration budgets for control and treatment. Preserve the existing LJ-only model and train an additional matched-budget LJ-only control where affordable, because the original checkpoint has different exposure and possibly different optimization. If Hi-Fi arrives late, prefer a stable, traceable 54 h + LJ pilot to interrupting a good run.

**Optimizer starting point (hypothesis):** AdamW, backbone LR 3e-5 and new speech heads/embeddings 3e-4 (the existing recipe), 3–5% warmup, cosine decay to 10–20% of peak, gradient norm clip 1, weight decay 0.01 on eligible weights, no decay on biases/norms/embeddings. Use length-bucketed batches, e.g. microbatch 4–8 and accumulation to ~16–32 utterances or ~8k–16k nonpadding sequence positions/update, subject to actual memory and speed. Count actual tokens: a frame with 4 or 8 parallel codebook targets is *one* transformer sequence position, not 4 or 8. Log both valid speech frames and total nonpadding positions, plus actual padded/processed throughput. New heads may require their own clipping and LR; take a 15-minute loss/NaN test before committing. Fine-tune initially at 1e-5 backbone / 1e-4 heads, warm up for ~100 updates, tune on validation only. These are not borrowed published optima.

**Regularization and alignment:** retain the current 15% feedback-code corruption only as a matched control; test 0–5% on a small fixed pilot and monitor free-running as well as teacher-forced loss, since corruption may improve exposure robustness while making a small dataset harder to fit. Evaluate dropout 0.05–0.1 if supported, early-stop at validation degradation, shuffle by speaker/book, and use replay. Do not conflate overfitting with token loss that is irreducible under the chosen parallel-head factorization. Mask PAD and delay positions correctly; confirm EOS target, codebook offset, teacher-forcing shift and text-to-audio attention mask with tiny overfit tests. In a causal sliding-window decoder, verify text remains visible to the relevant late generated positions: only global layers may retain distant text. If long clips fail selectively, test shortening and repeated conditioning or an explicit text cross-attention branch. T5-TTS's monotonic prior plus alignment loss improved its published intelligibility, but it required correct initialization; naïve alignment without the prior failed in that paper. It is architecture evidence, not a ready-made fix for Gemma.

## What published training recipes actually say

| Model/data | Reported training and inference | Relevance and limits |
|---|---|---|
| [T5-TTS](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2024/neekhara24_interspeech.pdf), encoder-decoder, 220M parameters **excluding embeddings**, ~1.8k h | AdamW, fixed LR 1e-4, batch 192 utterances, 250k updates, 32 A100s; dropout 0.1; top-k 80, temperature 0.85. Uses a text-pretrained T5 and explicit alignment. | Roughly 48M utterance presentations, not a published epoch count; 1.8k h is much larger than our available input. Its effective batch and 250k steps cannot be assumed feasible here. |
| [S5-TTS](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.21882), 160M, 845.04 h real | 4 B200s, microbatch 32 × accumulation 4 = effective 128 as reported; 250k updates, AdamW warmup 10k to 2e-4 then cosine to 1e-4; 100k distillation updates at fixed 1e-4; top-k 80, temperature 0.85. | The paper reports 3,827.50 h / 1.3M synthetic samples in addition to 845.04 h real. Different 86.1 frame/s codec, compute and alignment mean settings are references, not a prediction for Mimi. |
| [SpeechT5](https://aclanthology.org/2022.acl-long.393/) | Pretrained speech/text encoder-decoder with a continuous speech output, not a codec LM; TTS fine-tuning described with inverse-square-root schedule, max 1e-4, 6k warmup and 60k steps. | Useful transfer-learning precedent only, not an equivalent discrete-code recipe. |
| [VALL-E](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.02111) / [BASE TTS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.08093) | VALL-E pretraining: 60k h; BASE explores 100M–1B scale and 100k h. | Widely cited zero-shot and scale results depend on orders of magnitude more data. No defensible universal number of hours or steps can be inferred for our architecture. |

Training epochs depend on actual unique accepted duration, sampling weights, clipping and updates. With 54 h multi-speaker + 19 h LJ, one unique pass at 12.5 frames/s is ~3.285M audio frame positions; 346 h + 19 h is ~16.43M. These exclude text, padding, prompts, special tokens and rounding. With 19 h alone one pass is ~855k audio positions. Repeated presentations are not new data. [S5-TTS](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.21882) reports 250k × 128 = 32M example presentations but not enough mean duration or bucketing information to derive exact epochs. Judge convergence by held-out free-running quality and exposure, not nominal step counts.

## Synthetic data and distillation

S5's full-context T5-TTS teacher and student were first trained on 845.04 h of LibriTTS and Hi-Fi. The authors synthesized conversational text with training-speaker references, retained only zero-WER outputs using Parakeet-TDT 0.6B, yielding 1.3M utterances / 3,827.50 h. They interleaved real-audio teacher-forced soft labels with sampled text-only soft-label distillation; their LibriTTS WER/UTMOS for lookahead 2 moved from 3.49%/3.66 to 2.65%/3.72. This is a teacher/student streaming experiment with a capable teacher, not evidence that 3.8k h can be produced this weekend or that hard-label self-training reproduces it. [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.21882).

Only if an already-working local teacher and codec fit the budget: sample 30–60 minutes of *novel training-only text* with diverse target-voice references; store text, seed, teacher/version, prompt ID and waveform, screen with an independent ASR, listen to a stratified subset, encode in the student's codec, and mix initially <=20% synthetic speech seconds into real data. No test text or references. Compare with the same training compute and sampling schedule on real data; retain teacher and rejection artifacts. Teacher-generated acoustic labels are **hard pseudo-labels**, not S5 soft-label KL distillation unless matched tokenization and teacher distributions are actually available. Measure voice drift, duration and perceptual artifacts; feedback loops and the judge's systematic errors can be amplified.

## Decoding, reranking and RL

Freeze a validation-only decoding grid: greedy and temperature 0.2 (the current local winner), 0.4 and 0.7, with no truncation versus top-k 40/80 or top-p 0.9; don't assume all combinations fit the budget. Use identical checkpoints, text, seeds and caps. Log EOS, hard frame cap, truncated/empty clips, duration, duplicate-token loops and per-codebook settings. Temperature 0 means greedy, so top-k/p are irrelevant there. Sampling at 0.85/k80 is the T5/S5 published setting, not necessarily good for an undertrained Mimi model.

[VALL-E 2](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/articles/vall-e-2-enhancing-the-robustness-and-naturalness-of-text-to-speech-models/) samples with nucleus and, when the proposed token recurs too often in a recent history window, redraws from the full distribution. [CosyVoice's implementation](https://github.com/FunAudioLLM/CosyVoice/blob/main/cosyvoice/utils/common.py) instead excludes the repeated candidate before redrawing and provides defaults p=0.8, k=25, window 10, threshold 0.1. These are distinct algorithms and token spaces. Treat such settings as an ablation on the first/semantic stream only, not a blanket penalty on all residual codebooks: repeated acoustic codes and silence can be valid. Listen for buzz and broken vowels and count EOS failures.

Classifier-free guidance requires a trained unconditional or condition-dropped branch: `logits = uncond + s * (cond - uncond)`; in this convention s=1 is ordinary conditional prediction. If trained with text-conditioning dropout (e.g. 10% as a hypothesis), try s=1, 1.25, 1.5 and 2 only after verifying the unconditional branch; doubling inference passes has a cost. Speaker, text and prompt dropout need explicit semantics. Do not apply CFG to the existing model and claim meaningful guidance without such training.

**Best of N:** on development prompts, generate N=1, 2, 4 candidates with seeded nonzero-temperature sampling; transcribe candidates with Parakeet, normalize identically and choose minimum edit distance, with deterministic ties and hard invalid-audio/duration checks. Record all candidates, selection rate and wall/GPU cost. This uses the known desired text as a *selection* signal, so compare N=1 and N=4 with both quality and 4× synthesis plus scoring cost. Report WER with held-out Whisper-large-v3 and blinded listening; reranker's own WER is an optimized score and must not be presented as an independent test. Freeze N and settings before final test.

**RL:** [TTS-1](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.21138) used GRPO with 8 responses/prompt on a 1,000 h subset, Whisper-large-v3 WER, WavLM speaker similarity and DNSMOS rewards, plus clipping and KL anchoring to the SFT model. Their WER reward is `exp(-2.5 * WER)`; composite reward is not UTMOS. [WaveGrad2 RL](https://etd.ohiolink.edu/acprod/odb_etd/ws/send_file/send?accession=osu1723687468050122&disposition=inline) explored UTMOS reward for a diffusion TTS model, not this discrete AR architecture. A later experiment could score N=4 generated codec sequences with a bounded ASR plus UTMOS reward, a KL penalty to frozen SFT, and held-out second judges/listening; validate that the log-probability calculation matches the sampler and all heads. Reward hacking, reward-model errors, decoding/backprop complexity and rollout cost make this a no-go for the core weekend unless supervised alignment already works. Report reward and independent metrics separately.

## Evaluation protocol

1. **Freeze splits and baselines:** current project-27 100 LJ test texts, untouched LJ validation for selection, a separately reserved difficult-text set (numbers, names, long inputs, repeated words) and speaker-disjoint LibriTTS/VCTK if evaluating generalization. Check source overlap against training. For speaker-ID-only models, evaluate known-speaker LJ; do not describe unseen-voice synthesis as supported. Same texts, output sample rate, deterministic normalizer, frame cap and seeds across models; log any differences in conditioning and cost. Include original held-out LJ recordings, 4/8/16/32-codebook Mimi round trips, current Gemma and project 27. Rescore all with identical independent judges; do not compare prior biased CTC scores as cross-model evidence.
2. **Intelligibility:** use pinned Whisper-large-v3 in English/transcribe mode on each full clip without target-text prompt, fixed decode/no-speech settings, and retain transcript and errors. [Seed-TTS-eval](https://github.com/BytedanceSpeech/seed-tts-eval) uses Whisper-large-v3 for English and WavLM-large speaker verification cosine; it releases target/prompt/reference metadata, and describes 1,000 English Common Voice samples and 2,000 Chinese samples. Inspect the actual frozen manifest/version instead of relying on a secondary source's different item counts. For faithful Seed comparison use its exact normalizer, checkpoint and aggregation script; report separately if MLX implementation or quantization differs. Report corpus WER = total (S+D+I)/total reference words, mean utterance WER, CER, error counts, failure/truncation rate and per-clip records. A blank output is a deletion failure, never excluded. Whisper can hallucinate on silence; listen to suspicious cases and cross-check Parakeet, but preserve raw outputs. Report bootstrap paired 95% confidence intervals by utterance (and by speaker for multi-speaker results).
3. **Quality:** UTMOS22 strong predicted MOS and/or UTMOSv2 as named, fixed versions; report mean, median and spread, not human MOS. DNSMOS is primarily a signal/noise quality predictor and is not interchangeable with naturalness. Use randomized, level-matched, blind paired listening on at least 20 held-out clips covering easy, hard and long; score preference, intelligibility and obvious artifacts, record listener count and ties. Real and codec-roundtrip references calibrate both judges and listeners. Do not cherry-pick sample clips.
4. **Speaker similarity:** cosine similarity of a fixed speaker-verification model, generated clip vs a *different*, held-out same-speaker reference, ideally multiple reference clips; compare real-to-real baseline and different-speaker impostors. Seed's WavLM-large checkpoint is the protocol for direct comparability. Embedding similarity is not proof of speaker identity or naturalness. For one LJ voice, this is secondary to intelligibility and naturalness.
5. Record RTF, first-audio latency if streaming, output duration, peak memory, GPU slot time and failure counts alongside all quality measures. Save WAV, text, seed, checkpoint/config hashes, normalizer, ASR and judge versions, per-item JSONL and aggregate results. Freeze the validation-selected model and decoding configuration before running the untouched LJ 100 once; exploratory test use invalidates its status as a final holdout.

## Weekend execution and budget

**Accounting assumption:** 15k *nonpadding transformer positions/s* sustained during training, not generated waveform samples or all codebook predictions; verify on the actual multi-speaker batch and 4/8-head model. At this rate 1 GPU-hour is 54M processed nonpadding positions; 8k positions/update is ~6,750 updates/hour, and 16k is ~3,375 updates/hour, only if throughput includes the full forward/backward/optimizer cycle. A 2 h block is ~108M positions and 6,750–13,500 updates. Text, padding and prompt overhead lower usable audio-frame count. Memory pressure, thermal throttling, I/O, judge work and competing jobs may reduce this considerably. `BACKGROUND.md` reports 11–15k sustained for its fine-tune; it does not prove the same rate for every recipe. Measure actual total/nonpadding positions, steps, wall and GPU slot time; never calculate epochs from the optimistic number without those logs.

All long jobs use the shared GPU slot wrapper, CPU threads <=4, and individual runs <=45 minutes under the supplied Joy Lab rules, despite the background's suggested 2–3 h blocks. Implement 2 h stages as resumable 30–40 minute locked jobs. Before a >20-minute run, verify save/kill/resume with weights, optimizer, update count, scheduler, RNG, sampler state and config; compare uninterrupted vs resumed loss/next batch. Resolve the shared venv and lock paths from the actual project layout; the user-provided relative examples may be relative to the numbered project, not this nested `research/` directory. No concurrent training jobs on the same intended GPU budget; permit other lab jobs their slots.

If extra data are unavailable, use the 12 h for the best controlled continuation and preserve a same-compute control, not synthetic-data generation on speculation. If wall-clock weekend or shared slots cannot deliver 48 GPU-hours, stop at the highest completed gate; CPU preparation and queues are not free time. No RL or T5Gemma port is required for success.

**Go/no-go, precommitted:** run the final comparison if outputs are valid and independently scored even when the model loses; a proposed win requires lower paired Whisper WER than project 27 on the same 100 texts, a confidence interval and blind listening showing no clear deterioration in naturalness, with failure rates reported. This is a local 100-clip result, not a universal superiority claim. The codec round-trip is a diagnostic reference, not a mathematical upper bound on generation or on a different ASR judge. If the independent judge cannot run, no WER-based win claim. If no viable checkpoint exists, retain failure samples, exact conditions and negative results.

## Recommendations for us

1. **Independent evaluation and codec ceiling first.** Impact: high, reveals whether apparent gains are real and where information is lost. Effort: 2–3 GPU-h if tools are ready. Risk: judge availability and bias; calibrate against original recordings and retain independent transcripts.
2. **Multi-speaker pretrain with IDs, then LJ replay adaptation, against a matched LJ-only control.** Impact: potentially high on data-limited generalization; effort: ~19 GPU-h for pilot/main/adaptation within the core budget. Risk: corpus leakage, slower convergence or degraded target identity. Report actual accepted hours and exposure.
3. **Fix alignment and EOS before adding codec streams.** Impact: high if skipped text is caused by masking, shift or text visibility; effort: a few small tests plus profiling. Risk: larger architecture changes eat the weekend. Apply T5-style monotonic cross-attention only as a follow-up or separately budgeted prototype.

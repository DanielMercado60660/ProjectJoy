# Gemma Voice: architecture and alignment research

Research snapshot: 2026-10-30. Scope: the weekend project in `BACKGROUND.md`; online sources are linked at the claims. Project-specific numbers below are **reported in the background**, not independently remeasured here. No model, port, experiment, timing, or listening test was run for this report. Proposed hyperparameters and effort estimates are hypotheses, not results.

## Decision

**Bet the weekend on the existing Gemma 3 270M decoder-only implementation, first with word-level alignment and a compact timing controller, and retain the current delayed-Mimi run as the control.** It already works end to end, and its 38.0% test CER versus 66.9% from random initialization is meaningful evidence that the pretrained backbone helps. The obvious next uncertainty is whether explicit local text progress fixes deletions and slurring without breaking prosody. Run a narrow alignment A/B before spending days on a new backbone.

T5Gemma 2 is the more elegant research comparator: its bidirectional text encoder and decoder's access to the entire encoder sequence are attractive for text-to-audio alignment, and NVIDIA's T5-TTS gives a concrete recipe. But porting and numerically verifying merged attention, weight mapping, masks, RoPE, and cache is a substantial, unmeasured upfront cost. It is **not** a ready-made separate cross-attention T5; its merged self/cross attention changes how an alignment prior and CTC loss must be applied. Start the port only in a time-box after the Gemma gate, or if the port is already independently verified. Neither architecture is guaranteed to beat project 27.

The current independent bottlenecks are (1) text progress/alignment and (2) acoustic representation/prediction. The reported 4-codebook Mimi round trip is 18.6% CER, versus 12.3% at 8, 9.4% at 16, and 8.5% at 32; real recordings score 6.9%. These are **judge-specific round-trip measurements**, not hard mathematical CER lower bounds: generated signals need not equal the original reconstruction, and the LJ-trained judge is biased. Still, they strongly caution against declaring victory based on 4-codebook intelligibility alone. Meanwhile later codebooks have poor teacher-forced accuracy, so adding them blindly does not solve the issue. Use a second ASR and listening checks for the same reference, round trips, baseline, and generated clips.

## What T5Gemma 2 actually is

Google's [T5Gemma 2 paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.14856) and [270M–270M model card](https://huggingface.co/google/t5gemma-2-270m-270m) describe a pretrained **encoder-decoder**, adapted from Gemma 3, then further pretrained on approximately 2 trillion tokens with the UL2 denoising mixture. “270M–270M” does **not** mean 540M unique text parameters: the paper gives approximately 168M shared text embeddings + 100M encoder non-embedding + 100M decoder non-embedding = **368M text parameters**, plus a separate 417M frozen SigLIP vision encoder. Omit vision for this project. The text embedding is tied across encoder input, decoder input and the original text output projection; new audio embeddings and heads are additional, separately initialized parameters.

Its Gemma 3-family blocks use grouped-query attention, Q/K normalization, RMSNorm around attention and MLP blocks, RoPE, and 5 local to 1 global attention layers; the paper specifies RoPE bases 10,000 local and 1,000,000 global. Both stacks inherit the 270M family dimensions in the background (18 layers, width 640, four query heads, one KV head, head dimension 256 and MLP width 2048); confirm every value against the exact downloaded checkpoint configuration rather than assume all tokenizer or masking details. The encoder is bidirectional: local layers remain windowed and global layers have full visibility, rather than all layers being globally bidirectional. The [reference implementation](https://github.com/huggingface/transformers/blob/main/src/transformers/models/t5gemma2/modeling_t5gemma2.py) implements separate bidirectional local/global encoder masks. The 128k context advertised by the model card is not evidence that this TTS setup is robust over that length.

For each decoder layer, using decoder states `X` and encoder states `H`, the same `Wq, Wk, Wv, Wo` serve both attention types: `Q = X Wq`, `K = [X; H] Wk`, `V = [X; H] Wv`; a single masked softmax normalizes over decoder and encoder keys together. Decoder history is causal/windowed as appropriate; encoder visibility follows the reference merged masks. Order `[X; H]` here follows the paper; a port must match its chosen implementation's actual order and offsets. This is not two independently normalized attention outputs and it has no separately initialized cross-attention sublayer. **For alignment loss, extract encoder-key logits and separately normalize over valid text positions; for forward attention, retain joint normalization.** Altering that normalization would no longer be the pretrained model. Verify reference position handling and cache numerically; do not infer cross-RoPE rules from the simple paper equation. The paper's global-only cross-attention ablation was rejected after an approximately 1.3 point degradation on a different model/evaluation; it does not prove the same drop for TTS. [Paper, §§2–3](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.14856).

**Decoder-only comparison.** A text prefix plus causal speech is easy to port and cache, and uses the working 270M checkpoint and training stack. The text representation is causal, and with 512-token local attention, old prefix text eventually leaves the five local layers' receptive fields; the global layers retain it within the model's context. At 12.5 speech frames/s, 512 audio steps is about 41 seconds, less when accounting for prefix and delays. T5's text is encoded once and available through merged attention at decoder layers; it pays encoder compute and retains encoder K/V caches. Exact performance, attention mask behavior on long clips, and memory on MLX need measurement.

## Alignment methods and exact recipes

### NVIDIA T5-TTS: prior plus CTC

The original [T5-TTS paper, arXiv 2406.17957](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.17957), is a pretrained T5 encoder-decoder that predicts codec codes, optionally with separately spoken speaker context. It trains on 1.8k hours, has 12 encoder and 12 decoder layers, 12 heads, width 768, FFN 4096, dropout 0.1, batch 192 across 32 A100s, AdamW fixed LR `1e-4`, and 250k steps. These are **paper-scale settings**, not a laptop recipe.

1. For each example build a near-diagonal, center-wider **2-D beta-binomial** matrix `P[T_audio, M_text]`, where rows are target audio steps and columns are target text positions; exclude context slices from the guided target region. Apply it to **all cross-attention heads of all decoder layers**. The paper expresses this as elementwise multiplication of the text-answer attention-score slice by `P`; check the precise score convention in the specific NeMo implementation before reproducing it—multiplying signed pre-softmax logits is not equivalent to multiplying probabilities, and may increase a negative logit's relative weight. A clean proposed implementation is `softmax(logits + log(clamp(P, eps)))`, equivalent to multiplying **unnormalized positive attention weights**, but that is an adaptation until verified against source. For merged attention apply the bias to text keys only and preserve the single joint softmax.
2. Keep `P` for **S1 = 8,000** steps, then linearly interpolate the matrix itself to all ones by **S2 = 15,000**: `P_s = ((S2-s)P + (s-S1)1)/(S2-S1)`. No prior after S2 or at inference, since target duration is unknown. The paper does not specify a numeric beta-binomial concentration or epsilon in the cited description; don't invent them. The schedule is measured in their optimizer updates and is not automatically transferable to our batch size.
3. Extract the answer-to-question attention logits, softmax **over text positions**, and use CTC to maximize the probability of the positional target `1,2,...,M` for each guided head, summing head losses. They tried all heads versus two per decoder layer and found no significant robustness difference; their reported experiments use all. The paper does not specify enough in its text to reproduce the CTC blank construction or a loss coefficient unambiguously: obtain those from the exact released recipe/source, including reduction and scaling, before calling any implementation an exact reproduction. Use positional labels, not phoneme IDs: two identical adjacent letters are distinct alignment positions. In standard blank-CTC, account for a valid blank class; alternatively implement a blank-free stay/advance forward-sum and label it an adaptation. Check `T >= M` for the blank-free form. For T5's original higher-frame-rate codecs this constraint is less restrictive than 12.5 Hz Mimi.
4. The paper reports that alignment loss **without** the prior produced monotonic but wrong maps and no speech; an early diagonal is not proof of content alignment. For seen LibriTTS, the same paper reports CER 4.01% without prior/loss, 2.56% with prior alone, 2.16% with prior and CTC for its encoder-context setup. These results are not transferable estimates for our smaller data and codec. [Paper §§3.3–4.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.17957).

Implementation risk specific to Gemma/T5Gemma: fused MLX attention may not expose differentiable probabilities. Materializing `[batch, heads, audio_steps, text_steps]` for just selected layers is feasible as a prototype, but memory and runtime are **not measured**. Separately re-compute selected Q/K logits from the same forward activations with the same masks and scaling; test their values against the actual attention. Preserve padded masks and per-example lengths. Compute the alignment DP in fp32 with log-sum-exp, compare to exhaustive enumeration on tiny cases, and check finite gradients. Availability of a suitable differentiable MLX CTC primitive has not been verified; implement/test the forward sum in MLX or omit the term for the first prior-only A/B.

### S5-TTS: lookahead masks, not a generic monotonic mask

[S5-TTS](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.21882) extends T5-TTS for **streaming**. Map phoneme positions to words `π(i)` and codec steps to the current word `A(t)`. For a lookahead of `k` words, its encoder mask lets word `w` see all words through `w+k`; its decoder-to-encoder mask lets frame `t` see text through `A(t)+k`. Both masks operate at every relevant layer. Its experiments use `k=2` as the trade-off; `k=3` worsened intelligibility in their setting. A naïve stack of local-lookahead encoder masks can leak further future words through intermediate layers; implement the paper's actual cache/streaming behavior and test the effective receptive field before claiming a hard bound.

To obtain `A(t)` for training, its auxiliary attention projects decoder codec embeddings and encoder phoneme embeddings through convolution/ReLU projections, computes `softmax(-δ ||q-k||²)` with `δ=5e-5`, applies MAS to log attention, and trains the auxiliary alignment with CTC. During incremental inference it tracks word completion flags; it does not have ground-truth future codec frames. Its later distillation interleaves teacher-forced paired speech and teacher-generated text-only examples filtered to zero ASR WER, optimizing hidden-state MSE, logit KL, and token CE. This is substantially more than simply installing a lookahead mask. It is valuable if streaming is the goal, but for this offline weekend, use word progress as a simpler hypothesis and defer full S5.

### Monotonic alignment search and duration conditioning

[Glow-TTS](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2020/file/5c3b99e8f92532e5ad1556e53ceea00c-Paper.pdf) uses MAS to find the highest-scoring monotonic path through a text-by-acoustic score matrix, moving `stay` or `advance` and visiting ordered text positions. Dynamic programming takes `O(T*M)`, then backtracking gives frame counts per unit. This is a **hard Viterbi path**, whereas the CTC/forward-sum objective sums paths. MAS is only as trustworthy as its score matrix; a learned encoder or externally forced word timestamps are needed. The original model uses a duration predictor at inference, not MAS on nonexistent target audio.

For our decoder LM, derive **word** boundaries from a forced aligner or validated existing timestamps, map seconds to Mimi frames with a documented floor/rounding convention, and inspect 50 diverse examples including pauses and repeated words. Train a duration predictor on word/text state with Huber or L1 loss on `log(1+d_frames)`; clamp predicted frame counts to sensible positive ranges and track the remaining word budget as an additional embedding. At training use real durations; at generation use predicted durations—**never ground-truth test durations**. If timed word spans would be zero frames, merge adjacent very short words or use sub-frame timestamps with explicit zero-duration representation; do not silently drop them. Predicting duration adds useful progress information but can import bad alignments, make speech stilted, or truncate/repeat words when predicted durations are wrong. Compare a version that merely exposes current word/progress against a hard stop/advance controller before enforcing it.

**Frame-rate constraint:** 12.5 Hz is 80 ms/frame; many phonemes and even some characters occupy less than one frame. Strict one-frame-per-character/phoneme MAS or positional CTC requires `T >= M` (and standard repeated-symbol CTC may require more), often false. Measure invalid fractions by length before training. Align words or phoneme groups, upsample alignment features with a defensible learned/interpolated 2× representation, or use an external higher-rate aligner. Do not just discard difficult samples or claim that upsampling creates information. Word timing is the safer first experiment for this codec.

### Kyutai delayed streams: input timing and learned word actions

In [Delayed Streams Modeling](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.08753), text and audio occupy a shared 12.5 Hz frame clock. Each word begins with a `WORD` token at its timestamp, then its subword pieces occupy successive frames, and unfilled slots are `PAD`. TTS shifts audio **16 steps = 1.28 s** behind the input text; embeddings of the input streams are summed. A separate output action predicts when the next word should be injected, because ground-truth timing is unavailable for new text; a lookahead text stream supplies a future word. The temporal model uses a smaller autoregressive **depth transformer** over the codebook axis. The paper also describes a separate two-frame acoustic/semantic delay; do not conflate this with the 16-frame text/audio delay. Their text alignment preprocessing uses word timestamps (including reconciled pseudo-labels), not free inference from untimed text. Collision/overlap between multi-token words on a low-rate clock needs an explicit policy.

For us this is a promising second experiment if word timestamps are already available: it makes text progress part of the causal sequence, retains a Gemma decoder, and the action head replaces oracle test timings. But exact DSM requires timing preprocessing, two text streams, output actions, shifted audio, and depth sampling; copying the 16-step delay alone does not reproduce it. A whole-sentence prefix plus a time-aligned local word feature is a cheaper **inference** of ours, not Kyutai's recipe.

## Codec factorization and sampling

Let `c[t,q]` be codebook `q` at 12.5 Hz frame `t`. All sequences need explicit BOS/EOS, pad masks, shifted targets and a flush for delayed tails. Supervise EOS once on a designated stream/control head; only stop after generated delayed codebooks for the final real frame are complete. Count loss on valid targets, not on padding.

| Strategy | Factorization and upside | Cost / risk here |
|---|---|---|
| **Delay pattern** | Predict streams in parallel at model step `s`, with `c[s-q,q]` and separate heads; embeddings of previously emitted streams are summed. Lower-index same-frame codes arrive in earlier autoregressive steps, so residual streams can condition on them. This is the [MusicGen pattern](https://huggingface.co/spaces/facebook/MusicGen/blob/main/docs/MUSICGEN.md), also used for T5-TTS RVQ. | Existing implementation; `T+Q-1` output steps, plus BOS shift. For 16 codebooks the last residual is delayed 15 frames = 1.2 s relative to the first. Head dependence, loss imbalance and slow tail convergence remain; inspect actual causal shift with a synthetic grid. |
| **Depth transformer** | Main temporal LM outputs `h_t`, then a smaller causal depth model generates `c[t,0]`, `c[t,1]` conditioned on earlier codes, etc. [Kyutai DSM](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.08753) uses this. | Better explicit same-frame dependencies, but Q sequential small-model passes/frame and an untrained extra model; may help weak cb1–15, not assured. Benchmark latency and compute. |
| **Parallel / multi-token** | All heads predict same-frame codebooks conditioned on past temporal history; no in-frame ordering unless extra conditioning is added. NVIDIA predicts independent FSQ codebooks in parallel in [T5-TTS](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.17957). Multi-token heads can predict several *future time* frames at once, but independence within a group is an approximation. | Simple and fast, but independent same-frame heads are a poor assumed fit for hierarchical Mimi RVQ; head accuracy needs measurement. FSQ evidence is not evidence for parallel RVQ. |
| **Single-codebook codec** | One discrete token stream makes ordinary autoregressive next-token prediction possible. [WavTokenizer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.16532) reports one codebook at 40 or 75 tokens/s; [Single-Codec](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07422) is another example. | A real alternative, not Mimi cb0: local Mimi cb0 round trip is 97% CER. 40/75 Hz is 3.2×/6× as many temporal steps as 12.5 Hz; codec round-trip, inference compatibility, data re-encoding and training time must be evaluated. MLX availability for a specific checkpoint is **not verified** here. |

**VALL-E 2's two mechanisms are distinct.** Its [paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.05370) groups **consecutive time codes from the first codebook**, sums group input embeddings, and emits multiple within-group outputs per AR step; subsequent codebooks come from a separate non-autoregressive model, sequentially by codebook. Group sizes 1, 2, 4, 8 were tested; 2 improved WER/DNSMOS in that setup. This is not grouping residual codebooks or predicting Mimi's entire code matrix in one step. Its repetition-aware sampling first samples the first-codebook candidate with top-p, computes its recent repetition ratio over `K=10` codes, and if the ratio exceeds `0.1`, resamples from the full predicted distribution. The paper evaluates top-p 0.0–0.8 in 0.1 increments. Some paper notation is inconsistent about window and threshold names; use the algorithm and stated experiment values. In our task, test RAS on cb0 only as a diagnostic against loops; constant or repeated acoustic codes can be valid for silence, and low-temperature sampling has already helped. Measure effects on pauses and naturalness, and do not assume this fixes skipped words.

## Text units

- **Characters:** finite, dense units and simple normalization; current character path has real evidence and is a sensible control. It must learn English spelling/pronunciation and is long enough to make 12.5 Hz strict alignment invalid for some examples. Preserve case/punctuation only if normalized consistently; quantify out-of-vocabulary handling.
- **Phonemes:** can reduce pronunciation ambiguity and are linguistically closer to acoustic timing; pronunciation dictionary/G2P, number normalization, homographs, punctuation and errors become external dependencies. In low data, this is a promising hypothesis, not a guaranteed win. Compare with and without stress; assess G2P failures manually. [Google's phonetic-realization work](https://research.google/pubs/improving-phonetic-realizations-in-tts-by-using-phoneme-aligned-graphemes/) explains the grapheme/pronunciation mismatch. Do not align each phone to at least one 80 ms frame without checking lengths.
- **Gemma BPE:** shortest sequences and pretrained subword embeddings, but whole-word/morphological tokens have sparse counts in 10.5k sentences and BPE spans vary acoustically. The background's character experiment is useful local evidence; it does not establish BPE cannot work. If compared, use identical normalized data, speaker, steps and evaluation. T5-TTS trains **one model with task prompts for both SentencePiece and phonemes**, which is evidence both can be supported, not a demonstration of their individual relative quality in our setting. [T5-TTS §3.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.17957).

Start with existing characters and word segmentation. If a pronunciation source is already installed and permitted, test a clean phoneme front end with the same word boundaries; otherwise record it as a dependency and do not install. Keep the Gemma tokenizer for original text; use a separate small trainable table for explicit character/phoneme/word-control tokens, without pretending newly added phone embeddings are pretrained.

## CFG and preference/RL alignment

[Koel-TTS](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.05236) trains with **10% dropout of both text and speaker/context conditioning** and uses `logits_cfg = logits_uncond + γ (logits_cond - logits_uncond)`, `γ >= 1`. Both branches must receive the **same sampled audio history** at every step and maintain their own condition-dependent KV caches. Paper examples use `γ=2.5`; sweep 1.0, 1.25, 1.5, 2.0, 2.5 on validation and choose once. Apply guidance per codebook before temperature/top-k; measure saturation and per-head entropy. The no-condition branch must be trained with exactly the null representation used at inference. When text is dropped, disable text-specific prior/CTC and do not leak target word identity/progress through the side channel. Speaker-only CFG and text-only CFG require corresponding dropout combinations and are not Koel's exact joint-drop recipe. CFG approximately doubles effective inference batch/forward work, not training by 2×. Our 0.035 RTF is the existing non-CFG figure, not a prediction of guided RTF.

Koel's preference data: for each text/reference speaker, sample `P` candidates using top-k 80 and temperature 0.7; score ASR CER and speaker-embedding cosine, Pareto-rank them; DPO pairs highest vs lowest and discards pairs where the chosen output is worse on either metric. RPO pairs top two with bottom two and weights preference by a normalized reward gap. They evaluate DPO `β=0.01` and `0.05`, with results sensitive to β; reported models use around 18k hours and distinct context/target utterances. [Koel paper §§2.4–3](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.05236). These results do not establish likely gains from a few thousand LJ pairs.

A feasible *later* local DPO pilot: freeze an SFT reference; from 500–1,000 **training-only** texts, draw 4 candidates each, save the exact sampled codec token arrays, log probabilities, seed, codec version and EOS; rank by independent ASR normalized CER, reject ties/small gaps and degenerate durations, and manually listen to a sample. Optimize `-log σ(β[(log πθ(y+)−log πref(y+))−(log πθ(y−)−log πref(y−))])`, starting `β=0.01` and mixing 10–25% SFT batches as our stabilization hypothesis. Include all valid delayed-stream and EOS log probabilities; choose and document whether sequence log-probabilities are summed or length-normalized and evaluate length bias. Avoid training on val/test transcripts or silently choosing winners with the final held-out judge. For single-speaker LJ, ASR-only rewards can favor robotic, unnatural or silent/short outputs; enforce duration and listening checks and evaluate with a second recognizer. Don't use ground-truth speech as a preference winner without clearly changing the comparison.

[F5R-TTS](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.02407) applies GRPO to a **flow-matching model**, first making its outputs probabilistic and using ASR WER plus speaker similarity as rewards; its reported 29.5% relative WER improvement is on its own zero-shot evaluation, not evidence that a discrete Gemma codec LM gets that improvement. For a discrete autoregressive policy, GRPO would sample groups, standardize relative rewards, use policy ratios/clipping and a reference/KL constraint over token trajectories; rewards require codec decode and ASR for every candidate, with extra implementation, latency, variance, and reward-hacking risks. Offline DPO is the smaller first preference experiment. Neither CFG nor preference learning replaces missing alignment/data.

## Concrete design 1: Gemma 3 decoder-only, weekend primary

**Layout.** Keep the working pretrained 18-layer decoder, frozen pretrained text embeddings initially, and 4-codebook Mimi delay model. For utterance text `x` and `T` codec frames, feed `[BOS, normalized character text, TEXT_END, AUDIO_BOS, delayed audio grid]`. The whole text is available in global layers. Add an **aligned current-word feature** to each audio-grid input embedding: a small trainable encoder/table over the word's characters, plus relative position or remaining-duration bucket and a null/transition symbol. Word identities/timing during training come from verified forced word boundaries; audio training is teacher-forced. Retain separate per-codebook input embeddings and output heads, and preserve the known working shifts. Have a dedicated word-advance/control head from the decoder audio hidden state, trained on the next frame's word-boundary action. At inference feed only the provided text, model-predicted advance actions, and generated audio; no reference timing. A next-word/lookahead feature of one or two words is optional but must be identical in train and inference. Gate advancement until the current word's minimum frame budget and expose a conservative max-budget fallback; these are experimental heuristics, not guarantees. The hard gate can skip acoustic nuance, so first evaluate the **soft** feature/action version and log actions against reference boundaries.

**Losses.** `L = mean_valid(sum_q w_q CE_q) + 0.1 CE_advance`, starting with the existing measured codebook weights and corruption settings as control. `0.1` is a proposed initial coefficient; tune according to gradient magnitudes and validation. EOS/control loss is masked/weighted explicitly. A duration-predictor ablation adds `0.1 × Huber(log1p(pred), log1p(ref))`; keep separate. Optionally add positional forward-sum alignment loss to a small selected set of global prefix-attention heads, computed over **word spans**, but only after the word timing experiment; ordinary frozen-prefix attention is not separate cross-attention. Add a gentle beta-binomial prior on word positions in the same heads during training, removing it before generation. This is a proposed adaptation, not direct T5-TTS reproduction. The 15% random feedback corruption is an existing choice, not a proven optimum; compare 0%/15% only after basic alignment works.

**Codebooks.** Begin with 4 to preserve the 38% baseline and compare under equal compute. Once alignment improves, compare 4 and 8 with the same initialization/data and time budget, then a separately budgeted 16-head continuation; report each codec's round-trip and per-head CE/top-1. If delayed residuals remain poor, prototype a 2–4-layer small depth transformer (e.g. width 256, four heads) **after** the 4/8 comparison, teacher-force lower same-frame codebooks during training and sample them during inference; compare a full inference path including its sequential compute. No claim it is already available in MLX.

**Implement first.** (1) Confirm timing annotations can be obtained without new installs and save word-to-frame mapping, zero-frame/collision and dropped-example statistics. (2) Smoke-test exact shift, last-frame flush, action labels, no future/target leak and correct predicted-action inference. (3) Run 15-minute same-seed baseline vs word-feature/action on identical data, then continue in resumable runs of at most 45 minutes each only if validation teacher-forced losses and free-running examples support it. If timestamps are unavailable, **do not use oracle duration**: instead run prior-only global-head word-span experiment or CFG dropout as the smallest viable A/B, and document the missing alignment source. No external timing dependency should silently consume the weekend.

## Concrete design 2: T5Gemma 2 encoder-decoder, scoped comparator

**Layout.** Encode `[BOS, normalized character or phoneme units, EOS]` bidirectionally using the exact pretrained encoder; decode `[AUDIO_BOS, delayed Mimi grids]` with the pretrained merged-attention decoder and shared encoder context. Use the same Mimi data, heads, decoder corruption and word segmentation as design 1. The new codec input tables/heads are separate from the tied pretrained text table. A speaker ID embedding can be added only when multi-speaker data is introduced; first match LJ single speaker. Do not add a separate T5-style cross-attention block and describe it as the original checkpoint.

**Alignment.** Start prior-only on word positions: aggregate selected text-key logits into word-span log-sum-exp, compute beta-binomial prior over `(target frames, words)` and apply a text-key log-bias in the joint merged-attention forward pass; anneal from full bias to zero. This is our probability-space adaptation. Keep text and causal audio jointly normalized, verify encoder keys remain visible under local masks, and distinguish text attention's conditional distribution from the amount of joint mass allocated to text. An auxiliary forward-sum over word positions uses `log_softmax(text-only logits)` with monotonic stay/advance in fp32; `L = mean_valid weighted codec CE + λ mean_selected_heads forward_sum`, with proposed `λ=0.05` initially and test `0, 0.01, 0.05`; equalize loss reduction, not raw coefficients from another paper. The null-text CFG branch receives no prior or alignment loss. Skip/handle invalid `T < number_of_words` explicitly; inspect their prevalence. If prior-only works and gradients are reliable, test adding the forward sum; see whether it improves free-running speech rather than merely diagonal attention.

**Codebooks and schedule.** Exactly the same 4→8 delay-pattern gates as design 1; optionally compare a depth transformer only after both have a robust baseline. A local trial schedule could keep full prior for the first 10% of updates and anneal to uniform by 30% (**ours**, not NVIDIA's 8k/15k). Inference never uses target duration to construct the prior. Use `3e-5` pretrained backbone and `3e-4` new params only as the existing team's starting point, not a validated T5 recipe; evaluate validation loss and overfitting.

**Implement first.** Port the text-only checkpoint: map all names and shared tensors; implement bidirectional local/global encoder masks, decoder merged masks, Q/K norm, RoPE, cache and audio heads. Before speech training, compare encoder states, decoder logits and cached versus full forward against an independent reference on fixed short and >local-window text inputs, including padding and multi-step decoding. The background's `2e-4` Gemma port agreement is not evidence for this port; set and report actual fp32 tolerances. A PyTorch reference for conversion/numerical checking, if locally available and permitted, is not a training path. If no reference/checkpoint exists locally, record the gap instead of implying the port is ready. Time-box this verification to 2–4 hours as a planning estimate; stop if it slips. Compare against design 1 using **equal GPU time and identical splits, codec, speaker, LR search budget, and evaluation**, and also report throughput and parameter counts. Matching updates alone is not matching compute.

# Speech representation for Gemma Voice

Research date: 2026-10-30. Scope: Gemma 3 270M in MLX, one M5 Max, LJSpeech first; a weekend of experiments. This is a research recommendation, not a result from new audio runs. No model, dependency or data was downloaded for this report. All numbers below are published or supplied in `BACKGROUND.md`, unless explicitly called a calculation or proposal.

## Decision

**First choice to test: the released 16 kHz, 50 Hz BiCodec semantic stream, with one fixed speaker conditioning vector/token sequence extracted from a separate LJ training reference.** Generate its single 8,192-way code per frame with Gemma, and use BiCodec's decoder to render it. It has a strong published speech reconstruction result, a much smaller vocabulary than XCodec2, and no pile of unpredictable fine RVQ levels for Gemma to predict. Spark-TTS demonstrates this representation with a 0.5B Qwen LM, although that is not proof that 270M and 19 hours suffice. The published reconstruction uses speaker information from the target utterance; a fixed, non-target reference may lower the practical ceiling. Check that before choosing it. [Spark-TTS paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.01710), [Spark model](https://huggingface.co/SparkAudio/Spark-TTS-0.5B).

**Second, run a genuinely competitive continuous baseline:** Gemma autoregressively generates chunks of project 27's exact log-mel features, followed by the matching existing LJ vocoder. This meets the LM-generation requirement. A mel target avoids codebook classification and has an immediately measurable oracle-mel/vocoder ceiling. It might beat BiCodec on single-speaker LJ, but autoregressive regression can blur and drift; chunking and stop behavior must be tested. Preserve project 27's non-LM FastSpeech system as the external baseline.

**Third, test NanoCodec 0.6 kbps as a cheap MLX-native four-stream ablation**, not an assumed high-quality replacement: NVIDIA says this checkpoint is intended for limited-speaker fine-tuning and is not recommended for general encoding. If that ceiling fails, test the higher-rate 1.89 kbps checkpoint when a working encoder/decoder is available. XCodec2 and NeuCodec are high-ceiling single-stream challengers but their 65,536-way heads and port/encoding costs are material. Exact ranking must be decided by paired held-out data and generated speech, not the literature alone.

Our supplied evidence: real LJ 6.9% CER; Mimi reconstruction 18.6%/12.3%/9.4%/8.5% for 4/8/16/32 books; 4-book Gemma 38.0%; project 27 6.2%, all on the biased LJ recognizer. These are local historical measurements in `BACKGROUND.md`, not new verification. The gap between 18.6% and 38% means codec quality is only one bottleneck: alignment, limited data and prediction errors also matter. A reconstruction score is an **oracle-code round trip**, not a mathematical limit on the ASR score of generated speech: a decoder may denoise or produce a different utterance. Do not infer human quality or true CER ordering from the LJ proxy alone.

## Reading the table

`fps` is temporal frames per second of audio. `symbols/s` counts all discrete IDs emitted, including parallel heads; `AR steps/s` counts sequential backbone decisions if the stated schedule is used. For fixed-rate single streams they are equal. A delay-pattern model emits several IDs per backbone step, so dividing measured throughput by serial symbol counts is incorrect. For a level of size K, nominal fixed-width bitrate is `fps × ceil(log2 K)` bits/s per level, unless stated otherwise. No entropy coding, headers, speaker side information or padding are included. NVIDIA reports approximate capacity-based rates. When sampling a tuple of concurrent heads, joint independence conditional on the common history is an architectural assumption, not a free reduction in prediction difficulty. FSQ groups are not necessarily residual stages.

Published figures are **reconstruction**, not TTS. Most rows marked **S** use the same [Spark paper Table 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.01710), LibriSpeech test-clean, and include STOI / wideband PESQ / UTMOS. This is a convenient common comparison but codecs differ in sampling rate, bitrate and conditioning; higher UTMOS alone is not a guarantee of faithful waveform or intelligibility. Other studies use different test sets and metrics and must not be ranked numerically against S. `—` means not found or not applicable, not zero. Sample rates describe the cited checkpoint/configuration, not every release in the family.

For a direct sense of scale: at 640 hidden dimensions a new 8,192-way BiCodec output matrix is ~5.24M weights; separate input embedding adds another ~5.24M. A 65,536-way XCodec2/NeuCodec matrix is ~41.94M each, ~83.9M untied total, excluding biases/optimizer. Tying, low-rank heads or factoring FSQ may reduce this but change the model; measure loss, quality and throughput. About 19 h at 50 Hz supplies 3.42M frames, averaging ~418 examples per BiCodec ID or ~52 per 65,536-way ID **only under a uniform distribution**; actual code frequency is long-tailed. A single ID is not necessarily easier merely because it is single. Log occupancy, conditional NLL and per-code performance.

**Optional lead:** [25 fps / 24 kHz XCodec2 derivative](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.20185) has [released weights](https://huggingface.co/Scicom-intl/xcodec2-25TPS-24k); it halves backbone steps and improves nominal output bandwidth. Exact codebook/config, weight license, Mac/MLX usability and LJ ceiling need verification first; card does not declare a license. Do not assume it has the same quality or token IDs as original XCodec2.

A decoder's frequency response and conditioning matter. 16 kHz output is limited to <8 kHz acoustic bandwidth even if perceptually plausible; upsampling it to 24 kHz does not restore information. Conversely a higher sample rate does not guarantee greater perceptual quality. BiCodec 25 Hz results in the paper are promising (8,192: STOI .89, PESQ-WB 2.05, UTMOS 4.13 on LibriSpeech test-clean), but availability of that exact pretrained variant is unverified; simply dropping every other 50 Hz code is invalid. [Spark appendix](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.01710).

## How to measure the ceiling on our data before choosing

The following is a proposed experiment; none of it has been run for this report. Follow the fixed Joy Lab protocol: LJ001–040 train, LJ041–044 validation, LJ045–050 reserved test; choose representation and settings on validation only, and score the designated 100 test clips once for the shipped checkpoint. The supplied 100-clip test results are context, not a tuning set. For multi-speaker experiments keep speaker/reference selection and splits independent and report separately.

1. **Build one frozen validation manifest.** Use all eligible LJ041–044 clips, or a predeclared deterministic 200-clip subset stratified by length (1–3, 3–6, 6–10 seconds) if time is tight. Save utterance IDs, normalized text, original paths, sample counts, duration and hashes. Fix a small set of training-chapter LJ speaker-reference clips with no target audio or text. Pin model revision, codec implementation, preprocessing, device/dtype, codebook count and seed; store these in a JSONL manifest. Keep each codec's native sample rate; mono downmix, resample once with the same specified polyphase filter, preserve gain and do not silently trim. Include a resample-only control.
2. **Encode then decode ground-truth audio.** Save PCM WAVs at native output rate, tokens as `.npz` integer arrays with original lengths and a JSONL record. For Mimi test 4/8/16/32; BiCodec save semantic IDs and separately evaluate (a) same-utterance oracle globals, and (b) one fixed reference from training. Only (b) corresponds to the intended fixed-voice system. For XCodec2, NeuCodec, NanoCodec, SNAC, WavTokenizer, DAC, EnCodec and X-Codec, use each actual published checkpoint and supported full encoding/decoding; don't synthesize a new rate by dropping tokens unless the checkpoint supports it. For CosyVoice use ground-truth tokenizer IDs plus its exact flow, speaker conditions and vocoder; run fixed seeds and also record target-conditioned vs fixed-reference prompts, as the former includes target side information. For VibeVoice encode and decode original continuous latents (mean and, separately, stochastic samples with fixed seeds). For mel feed exact ground-truth mels to the compatible frozen vocoder. Save full-precision references and outputs; note any stochastic sampling.
3. **Check fidelity of the evaluation pipeline.** Verify duration and correct sample rate, padding/cropping and latency using a known waveform; only compensate documented codec delay before *paired* waveform metrics, and report the shift. Do not time-warp. Confirm each encoder token shape/range, valid last-frame lengths, output amplitudes/clipping and decode repeatability. Compare MLX implementation against the official implementation on identical small inputs/codes, allowing numerical tolerance but checking that disagreement does not change decisions. If an encoder or decoder cannot run under existing constraints, mark that candidate unavailable rather than inventing a score.
4. **Score the same clips with multiple views.** First, preserve project 27's fixed recognizer pipeline and report corpus CER, raw edit counts, and paired difference vs the original and Mimi. Run an independent, fixed Whisper-class or other ASR model on original and reconstructions with identical text normalization; report corpus WER/CER, not only differences vs ASR transcript. Compute per-utterance STOI and PESQ-WB on correctly aligned 16 kHz pairs if the implementations are already available; otherwise explicitly mark unavailable (do not install). UTMOS is optional and model/version-dependent. Report speaker similarity and F0 only if validated tools are available. Include reference native-bandwidth and 16-kHz-band-limited controls, because metrics at 16 kHz cannot judge >8 kHz detail. Compute paired bootstrap confidence intervals over utterances (e.g. 2,000 resamples, seed 0); report catastrophic transcript errors, clipping, lengths and failures, not just means. Do a randomized, level-matched blinded A/B/X listening panel including originals, Mimi 4 and 32, BiCodec fixed vs oracle globals, and mel-vocoder; predefine intelligibility, naturalness, timbre and artifacts, and retain bad examples. ASR may favor recognizer's training domain; UTMOS is a predictor and can reward plausible but inaccurate audio. [Metric study](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.19734).
5. **Set a validation gate before looking at scores.** Example pragmatic screen: independent-ASR excess WER no more than 2 absolute percentage points vs original, no material degradation on LJ-CER vs Mimi-32, no recurrent audible consonant loss, and no intolerable voice drift on fixed-reference reconstruction. This is a proposed threshold, not a proven target; retain confidence intervals and listen to failures. If no codec passes, do not declare one successful. Prefer the feasible passing candidate with best fixed-reference quality and model predictability, not highest theoretical bitrate or reconstructed UTMOS. Reserve test set until the final choice is frozen.

### Predictability is a second gate

An oracle-code round trip alone cannot tell us what the 270M will learn. After the ceiling screen, run short, matched budget pilots on the same train/val sentences, same Gemma initialization and text preprocessing, same training wall-clock/accelerator budget, reporting both audio seconds and optimizer updates. Use identical checkpoint selection and fixed sampling rules on validation. Include current Mimi-4 and the continuous mel baseline. Do not assume matching epochs means matching compute: 50 fps produces 4× as many temporal positions as 12.5 fps, and full attention, output projections and memory differ.

For each discrete candidate report validation NLL in bits **per second of audio** as well as per symbol and per codebook, top-1/top-k, class occupancy, EOS errors, audio-seconds/s, wall time, peak memory and logits/embedding parameter count. Cross entropy across different code vocabularies is not a standalone ranking of perceptual ease: alphabets, loss weighting and code stochasticity differ. For parallel heads sum unweighted per-head log losses before computing bits/s; retain task-specific weighted objective separately. For mel report normalized per-bin L1/L2, predicted length, flux/F0 behavior and rollout quality, not cross-entropy. Teacher forcing is diagnostic; evaluate free-running decoded samples on validation for independent-ASR CER/WER, duration ratio, skipped/repeated words, stop/loop rates, listening and RTF. Use at least two seeds if time permits and preserve raw failures. Generate the same prompts with each model and count all backbone, auxiliary decoder and vocoder inference time.

A BiCodec-specific diagnostic is particularly valuable: decode **true content IDs with fixed reference globals**. If this sounds poor or causes large intelligibility loss, changing Gemma will not fix the conditioning mismatch. Then compare teacher-forced loss and autoregressive generation; never present oracle target globals as a zero-shot/fixed-voice result. For NanoCodec evaluate all 4 heads and test conditional dependencies; a semantically coherent leading stream is not guaranteed. For Mimi, an optional small within-frame acoustic depth predictor can exploit correlations between books but must be trained and evaluated with its full cost. Both options remain hypotheses.

## Concrete first pilot

- **BiCodec**: use exactly the released 16 kHz/50 Hz checkpoint, one 8,192-class speech head and speech input embedding, keep text embedding initially frozen as in round 1. Let Gemma autoregress one content code/frame, prepend a fixed speaker condition from a training reference or feed it only to the decoder if the adapter supports that. Use explicit BOS/EOS and valid-token masks. Begin with the established text encoding, LR 3e-5 backbone / 3e-4 new embedding and head, bf16, a 15-minute smoke/pilot; these are starting hypotheses, not tuned optima. Avoid 262k-way full text logits for speech-only output. Save validation samples and auditable tokens. Reference choice, target extraction, dropout/corruption and sampling must be frozen before the test.
- **Mel**: precompute project 27's exact 80-bin log-mels; standardize using **training-only** per-bin statistics and save them. Group 4 consecutive frames, pad/mask last group, predict 320 normalized values from each Gemma hidden state with a small linear/MLP head; feed back previous ground-truth chunk during teacher forcing and model output during rollout. Add a separately trained EOS head, mask padded frames in L1 loss, and report a spectrogram-flux term as a controlled follow-up. Inference uses the same saved normalization and matching vocoder. Evaluate both teacher-forced oracle-length and free-running predicted-stop outputs. A single chunk cannot solve long-range text alignment by itself; inspect skipped words and EOS.
- **Compute discipline:** take checkpoints with weights, optimizer, step, RNG and config; verify save/kill/resume before any run >20 minutes. Respect the supplied per-run 45-minute limit unless the user explicitly extends it; a weekend total is not by itself an extension. Jobs longer than ~1 minute use the actual project GPU slot wrapper and CPU threads ≤4. The brief's relative `../bin/gpu_run` and `../.venv` paths are relative to the project folder, not automatically this `research/` subdirectory; verify paths from the execution directory. No installation or model downloads were authorized for this research task, so these are future steps rather than commands claimed to work now.

## Recommendations for us

1. **BiCodec single-stream + fixed LJ reference; gate it with paired reconstructions first.** Expected impact: best first balance of published reconstruction and smaller prediction space; removes Mimi's fine-RVQ burden. Effort: medium, particularly full encoder/decoder access, MLX parity and conditioning. Risk: fixed-global ceiling may be materially worse than target-global paper scores; 50 Hz increases context/training cost and 270M/19 h may still fail alignment. No promise it beats the 6.2% baseline.
2. **Gemma chunked log-mel + existing LJ vocoder in parallel conceptually, subject to resource lock.** Expected impact: directly tests a high-ceiling continuous target and uses local vocoder; may be the strongest single-speaker path. Effort: medium, small head and feedback/stop handling. Risk: blurry autoregressive mel, exposure drift and errors from a mismatched feature pipeline. Its oracle ceiling must be measured, and 4-frame grouping must be compared with a smaller chunk if alignment fails.
3. **NanoCodec 0.6 as a fast low-rate controlled experiment; 1.89 as a quality fallback.** Expected impact: 12.5 temporal steps/s, four parallel heads and public MLX round trip make 0.6 an efficient diagnostic. Effort: low to medium if compatible code and checkpoint are already available; otherwise availability is a blocker under this task's no-download rule. Risk: NVIDIA's limited-speaker warning, unknown LJ ceiling, and multiple dependent targets. The 1.89 variant has stronger intended reconstruction but eight streams and unverified full Python-MLX encoding.
4. **XCodec2 or NeuCodec if BiCodec's fixed-reference gate fails.** Expected impact: strong published XCodec2 reconstruction or a NeuCodec 24 kHz output, with one stream. Effort: medium to high for compatible encoder/decoder and large head; consider tied or factored output as explicit ablations.

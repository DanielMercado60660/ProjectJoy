# Round 5 — Learn when to think longer

**Question.** Can a small mistake head allocate a frozen gen-191 policy/value net's search more efficiently than fixed MCTS or well-tuned classical gates? Predict a *value-losing move after an 8-simulation probe*, then spend extra simulations on the highest risks. The backbone is identical and frozen in every arm; this is an allocation experiment, not more AlphaZero training.

**Fresh data and scope.** Seeds 50241011/23/37 generate train/validation/test independently, one position per trajectory, balanced across plies 10–15, 16–23, 24–32. Half the trajectories use random moves, half a safe-move heuristic; terminal trajectories are rejected. Mirror-canonical keys exclude every retained `checkpoints/**/replay.npz`, the old solver split, and other new splits. Manifests retain replay hashes and seeds. Earlier overwritten replay buffers cannot be checked: we cannot certify absence from *all historical* replay. Each accepted board has exact weak values for every legal move within a 3M-node cap. Timeouts are retained in `unsolved.jsonl`; this defines a solvability-conditioned population, not unrestricted Connect Four. Freeze 384 validation and 384 test boards before adding training data. Never shrink validation to meet a deadline.

**Signals and tuning.** Inputs are board planes, priors, probe visits/Q, value and cheap tactical summaries. The MLX head has two ReLU layers, unweighted stable BCE, and six candidates: `(width, lr, decay)` = `(64,.001,.001)`, `(128,.0003,.001)`, `(128,.001,.01)`, each at seeds 51/73. Only rounds with equal update counts can select a head. Selection minimizes mean NLL across the complete validation set, each ply group, and drawn positions; empty groups are reported. Compare entropy, `1−abs(value)` (a heuristic, not epistemic uncertainty), prior/visit total-variation disagreement, a 576-setting rule grid (AB depths 2/4/6/8, three signals, four weights, four biases, three scales), and logistic regression on the head's exact inputs with six ridge strengths .0001–10, up to 400 L-BFGS iterations each. Rule inference pays for its selected alpha-beta depth; other gates do not compute it. All settings use train/validation only.

**Budgets.** Simulation arm: probe 8, fixed mean 32, adaptive maximum 128. Rank within each unlabelled batch and allocate an exact integer total of `32*N`, continuing the existing trees. Time arm: median of three fixed-arm timings on the same batch supplies the allowance; charge root expansion, allocation, features, predictor, sorting, search and move extraction. Deepen the highest-risk fraction until the deadline. Report actual time and reject an “equal-time” claim outside ±10%; compulsory overhead can exceed the allowance. This measures batched throughput, not interactive latency. No abstention: every position returns a legal move. Fresh paired game starts compare learned and validation-selected classical allocation against fixed play, both colours, identical mean simulations per decision; report actual total costs and incomplete games. Re-run all 1,000 old solver positions as a secondary simulation comparison.

**Measures.** Report value-losing moves, rescued and newly introduced errors, net errors avoided per millisecond and simulation, non-lost accuracy, and every ply/value group (including draws). Brier/NLL/ECE and reliability bins assess calibration. Raw policy and probe error counts provide references. Games retain moves, W/D/L, colours, costs and censoring; the offline HTML shows measured allocations and failure boards. No headline from partial evaluation or an unqualified time match.

**Measured-rate plan and custody.** Historical solver logs: 1,000 positions used 171.59 summed solve seconds; the new CPU-only 36-board probe took 0.62 s (small sample, no MLX). These are not end-to-end rates. Scale beyond a tiny fixed-step run: 24-minute main, 14.4-minute data window with live end-to-end boards/s projections, then six heads trained until the remaining guard using measured steps/s. Eight-minute evaluation expands paired game batches until its guard. Smoke ≤100 s gives a battery total about 34 minutes. Two alternating resume slots retain every candidate's weights/optimizer/step, NumPy/MLX RNG, immutable config and data hashes; data RNG is separately checkpointed. Only `selected/` needs committing. Smoke checks exact resume and actual save/SIGKILL/resume before the >20-minute run. No MLX execution by Astra.
